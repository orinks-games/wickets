# Dragon Dance

## About the game

Lunar New Year: you are the dragon's head. Keep your head in the pearl's lane on every beat, rear up on the gong, catch red envelopes, and avoid firecrackers. Streets always play to the end; up to 3 stars.

## The street and its lanes

A street has 3, 4 or 5 lanes, numbered from the left. Left and Right move your head one lane at a time, and each move says the lane you are in. At the edge of the street a wooden bump sounds and your head stays where it is. Your ears move with your head, so whatever is in your lane sounds straight ahead.

## Following the pearl

The pearl is a soft, glassy shimmer ahead of you, heard from its own lane: to your left, in front, or to your right. When it moves to another lane, a bright chime of three notes sounds from its new lane. It only moves on the first beat of a bar, and that beat is free: you have until the next beat to reach it. A soft high ping tells you your head has reached the pearl's lane. Tab rings the pearl's chime again and names its lane. With spoken call-outs on, each move is also named, such as "Pearl, lane 3."

## Beats, points and combos

The drum plays a deep beat, with a cymbal on the first beat of each bar. On every beat that your head is in the pearl's lane, the dragon dances and scores 1 point for each segment of its body. Beats on the pearl in a row build your combo. Every 16 beats in a row bring a big cheer and 50 points. One beat away from the pearl, to catch an envelope or dodge, keeps the combo. Two beats away in a row break it, with a falling tone.

## Gongs and rearing up

Drum roll: gong in 2 beats. Press Space right on the gong to rear for a perfect, 100 points; a little off is good, 60. A gong passed without a rear ends with a dull knock and scores nothing. Rearing when no gong is near breaks your combo, so wait for the roll.

## Red envelopes

A papery rustle with little bells is a red envelope coming down a lane. It is heard far ahead and comes closer over 4 beats. Be in its lane on the beat it reaches you to catch it: 40 points, and the dragon grows one segment longer, so every beat after scores 1 more. Many envelopes come down the pearl's own lane. Others come down the lane beside it: step over for that beat, then back. Up names what is coming and how many beats away it is.

## Firecrackers

A fizzing crackle is a string of firecrackers in a lane, coming closer over 4 beats. Keep your head out of that lane on the beat they reach you. If your head is in it, the tail stumbles over them with a clatter, and you lose 50 points and your combo. Firecrackers never end the dance, and they are never in the pearl's lane: stay with the pearl and you are safe. On the later streets they wait in the lane the pearl has just left, so follow quickly.

## Shops and crowds

Shops line both sides of the street, one every other bar. As you pass a shop, its crowd claps politely, or cheers if your combo is 4 or more, for 20 points. The cheer comes from the side the shop is on. With spoken call-outs on, a cheering shop is named, such as "The tea house cheers, on your left."

## Stars and the streets

The parade has 8 streets, from Market Row to The Grand Avenue. Each is faster and wider than the one before: Market Row has 3 lanes and no firecrackers, The Grand Avenue has 5 lanes and quick drums. At the end of a street you earn stars by your share of the best score the street allows: 35 percent for 1 star, 60 percent for 2, and 80 percent for 3. 1 star opens the next street. Play takes you to the next street without a star. Street select lists every street with your stars, and locked streets say what opens them.

## Practice street

Practice street is short and slow, with every call spoken. Stumbles cost nothing, and nothing is saved. It is a safe place to learn the sounds before the parade.

## Lunar New Year on the real calendar

The parade follows the real calendar. The year's animal follows the lunar year, which turns on Lunar New Year, so in January and early February it is still last year's animal. In the 10 days before Lunar New Year, lanterns go up over the shops with a bright little chime, lion-dance drums and cymbals play along with the drum, and red envelopes count double. The title plays its festival tune then. Lunar New Year dates are known from 2024 to 2040. After 2040 the festival days do not open, and the New Year parade and Lantern walk rows say so.

## The New Year parade

On Lunar New Year, and the day either side of it for time zones, the New Year parade opens on the title. On other days its row is locked and says how many days are left. The year's animal greets you with a line as the parade begins. It is a long street of 4 lanes with many red envelopes. It ends with a countdown from 5 to midnight, then the great gong: rear up on it for double points. Then firecrackers burst all down the street.

## Keepsakes

Dancing the New Year parade to the end earns a keepsake for the year's animal, such as a brass ox bell for the Year of the Ox. There are 12 animals, one each year, so collecting them all takes twelve New Years. Keepsakes, on the title, lists the ones you hold, the year you earned each, and the animals still to come, then the lanterns you have found.

## The Lantern walk

The Lantern Festival is the fifteenth day of the new year, 14 days after Lunar New Year. On that day, and the day either side, the Lantern walk opens on the title. It is a calm walk at a slow drum, on a quiet evening street, with no firecrackers and no gongs. Lanterns come down the lanes instead of envelopes, each with a warm, low glow of chimes: catch them as you would an envelope. There are 12 kinds of lantern, such as the rabbit lantern and the carp lantern, and each new one joins your collection.

## Challenges

Every player gets the same challenges. The daily is one street of 4 lanes and 24 bars, new each day. The weekly is the long parade, 48 bars with a gong every other bar, new each Monday. The monthly is the grand festival: 5 lanes, quick drums and firecrackers close behind the pearl, new each month. Your result counts when the street ends. Leave the challenge, on the pause menu, does not count, so you can try again later.

## Spoken call-outs

Spoken call-outs, a row in Settings from the title or the pause menu, switches the spoken calls in play. On, a voice names the pearl's lane when it moves, each red envelope, lantern and firecracker string with its lane as it starts to come, and each cheering shop. Off, you play by the sounds alone. Practice street always speaks its calls. Status, Up and the hint work either way.

## Status, what is coming and hints

S gives your lane, the pearl, everything coming with its lane and beats, and the next gong. Press S again straight away for your score, the dragon's length, your combo and the bar. Up names only what is coming. Down describes your dragon: its length, envelopes caught and combos. R repeats the last call. H gives a hint for this moment, such as which way the pearl is or what to do about firecrackers in your lane.

## Sounds

The drum is a deep drum on every beat, with a cymbal on the first beat of each bar. The count-in is wooden clicks. A drum roll warns of a gong, and the gong is a long, low metal ring. A perfect rear is a whoosh and a bright cymbal, a good rear a whoosh, a missed gong a dull wooden knock, and a rear with no gong a soft thud. The pearl is a glassy shimmer, and its move a bright chime of three notes. A red envelope is a papery rustle with little bells, caught with coins and a chime. Firecrackers fizz and crackle, and a stumble ends in a bang and a clatter. Crowds clap or cheer. The sound check on the title plays each one with its name.

## Controls

Left and Right: move your head one lane.

Space: rear up, as the gong strikes.

Tab: ring the pearl's chime and hear its lane.

Up: what is coming down the lanes, and the next gong.

Down: your dragon's length and combo.

S: your lane, the pearl, what is coming and the next gong. Press it again for score, length, combo and bar.

R: repeat the last call.

H: a hint for this moment.

F1: help for what you are doing right now. F2 stops speech.

Escape: pause, with Resume, Settings, Help, Back to the title, and Back to the lobby. In a challenge, Back to the title is Leave the challenge.

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's spoken instructions follow your bindings.
