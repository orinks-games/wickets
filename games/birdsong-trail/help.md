# Birdsong Trail

## About the game

Dawn trail: walk close until a bird sings to you. Whistle its song back note for note to befriend it into your field guide.

There is no way to fail and no clock. A walk ends when you reach the trail's end or head home. The songs are made for the game from three notes. They are not recordings of real birds.

## Finding your way

Left and Right turn you one eighth of a circle, and each turn names the way you face: north, north-east, east and so on. The trail runs north from where you start.

Every song comes from the bird's true place. A song in your right ear is to your right. One in both ears equally is ahead or behind, and a song behind you is a little softer and duller. Near birds are louder than far ones.

The trail's end makes its own sound the whole walk: a brook in the woodland, wind chimes on the meadow gate, frogs by the marsh's bird hide, and the weir on the river. Walk toward it to finish.

## Walking

Up takes a step forward and Down a step back. Hold the key to keep walking. Your steps sound like the ground: earth, dry leaves, long grass, a boardwalk, pebbles or snow. Steps are only for flavour. No bird minds how loudly you walk.

You hear when you leave the path and when you come back to it. Brambles, a hedge, deep reeds or the river's edge stop you at the sides of the trail, and the game says which way the path is.

## Meeting a bird

Walk toward a song. When you are within a few steps, the bird notices you, its name is spoken, and it sings its song to you alone. The other birds hush while it sings.

Space, when no bird is singing to you, names the three birds nearest you, where they are and how far, and the trail's end. H names the nearest bird you have not befriended, where it is, and how to turn to face it.

## Answering a song

Your turn: Down is low, Space middle, Up high (songs are 2 to 5 notes). Your whistle has its own soft sound so you can hear the answer.

If your answer matches, a warm rising chime plays and the bird is befriended. If it does not, two soft low notes play and the bird sings again. You can try as often as you like. After two misses, its notes are spoken in words.

While a bird waits for your answer, R asks it to sing again, and H speaks its notes in words, such as low, high, middle. Turning away, or stepping off, lets the bird be. Walk a little way off and come back, and it will sing to you again.

Turn on Spoken call-outs in Settings, from the title or the pause menu, to hear the notes of every song spoken after it.

## Seasons

The birds follow the real calendar, by the seasons of the northern hemisphere.

In spring the migrants arrive: swallows, warblers, the chiffchaff and the cuckoo. They sing through the summer and leave in autumn. Winter brings visitors from the north, such as juncos, waxwings, redwings and snow buntings. In winter the trails are quieter, with fewer birds and longer gaps between songs, and snow underfoot.

At the start of a walk, you hear which bird is new for the season, or which has gone.

## The trails

There are four trails. Each is a habitat with its own birds.

- Woodland trail: open from the start.
- Meadow trail: opens when your field guide holds 4 kinds of bird.
- Marsh trail: opens at 9 kinds of bird.
- Riverside trail: opens at 15 kinds of bird.

A walk holds 6 birds in spring and summer, 5 in autumn and 4 in winter. Birds with a song you have not learned yet come first.

## The field guide

Every bird you befriend goes into your field guide. Each singing bird has 3 songs to learn: its signature song first, then two variations, one each time you meet it again. A soft page turn and a bell mean something new went into your guide.

Read the guide from the Field guide row on the title. Each bird's page plays its song and reads how its song sounds, a line about the bird, how many songs you have learned, and where you first met it. The last row says how many birds are still to meet, and where to look.

## Special walks

Some days of the bird year open a special walk, on the Special walk row of the title. On other days the row is locked, and its help says which walk is next and how many days away it is. Each day counts from the day before to the day after, so it opens in every time zone.

- Dawn chorus, on International Dawn Chorus Day, the first Sunday in May: every bird of the season sings at once, and you pick them out one by one.
- Spring migration and autumn migration, on the spring and autumn equinoxes: flocks of geese, cranes and swans fly over, and you count each flock.
The migrating flocks can only be met on these walks.

## The dawn chorus

Up to 8 birds sing at once, each from its own place around you. You are asked to find one, and its song is described and played alone first. Turn with Left and Right to face where you hear it, and press Space to pick it.

The right bird plays a bright two-note chime and flies off, and another bird takes its place. A wrong pick names the bird you chose. R plays the song to find alone again, and H says where it is. Each bird you pick out goes into your field guide.

## The migration count

You stand on a hill as flocks fly over, from behind you to ahead in spring, and the other way in autumn. A rush of wings means a flock is coming. Each bird calls once as it passes. Count the calls.

When the flock has passed, Up and Down set your count, and Space gives it. R hears the flock again before you answer. An exact count is 3 points, one off is 1 point. An exact count puts the flock's bird in your field guide.

## The practice glade

The practice glade is a short path with two birds: a chickadee just ahead, and a wagtail to your right. Their songs have two notes each. Nothing you do there goes into your field guide.

## Challenges

The Challenges menu holds 3 challenges, the same for every player.

- Daily, Morning walk: a trail and 5 of its birds, each singing its signature song. 3 points for a bird answered first time, 2 after one miss, 1 after more.
- Weekly, Chorus week: 8 birds of the season sing at once. Pick out each one: 2 points first time, 1 after a miss.
- Monthly, The great count: 8 flocks of up to 12 birds. 3 points for an exact count, 1 for one off.

Each tells you its rule and when the next one comes before you start. Escape on that briefing goes back without starting. Reaching the end, or heading home, counts toward your streak. Leave the challenge, on the pause menu, does not.

## Records and results

When a walk ends, you hear which birds you befriended, the new songs, and your field guide's total. Then the results menu offers Play again, Hear the results again, the Field guide, and Back to the title. Escape does the same as the last row.

Records, on the title, reads your field guide's totals, your walks and steps, your best dawn chorus and migration count, and your challenge results.

## Sound check and tutorial

Sound check, on the title, plays each sound you need on the trail with its name and what it means: your whistle, each bird voice with its low, middle and high notes, the flock calls, each kind of step, the cues and the sounds at each trail's end. Tutorial explains the game a page at a time.

## Controls

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's spoken instructions follow your bindings.

- Left and Right arrows: turn. In the migration count, change your count.
- Up arrow: step forward. When a bird waits for your answer, the high note. In the migration count, count up.
- Down arrow: step back. When a bird waits for your answer, the low note. In the migration count, count down.
- Space: name the birds around you. When a bird waits for your answer, the middle note. In the dawn chorus, pick the bird you face. In the migration count, give your count.
- Tab: head home. Press it twice; any other key keeps walking.
- S: status. The bird you are with, which way you face, and where the trail's end is. Press it again straight away for the trail and season and the birds befriended.
- R: repeat. The bird sings again, or the last thing said is repeated. In the chorus, the song to find plays alone. In the count, the flock flies over again.
- H: hint. Where the nearest new bird is and how to turn to it, or the notes of the song in words.
- Escape: pause. The pause menu can resume, open settings or this help, go back to the title, or go back to the lobby. During a challenge, its leave row is Leave the challenge.
- F1: what the keys do on the screen you are on.
