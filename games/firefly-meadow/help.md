# Firefly Meadow

## About the game

Dusk meadow: fireflies blink as soft tones from where they drift. Walk close, press Space as one glows to jar it. A full jar set free lights the meadow and rings a chord. Nothing can go wrong, and outside challenges nothing is timed. Enter heads home.

This help gives distances in metres. Set Units to US in Settings and the game says feet instead.

## Walking the meadow

Up, Down, Left and Right walk one step at a time, and each step crunches softly in the grass. Up is always north. Hold a key to keep walking. A rustle of tall grass means the edge of the meadow, and the edge is named, such as "the north edge". Frogs, the creek and the trees each sound from one place all evening, so you can find your way by them.

## Hearing fireflies

Each firefly blinks as a soft tone. A tone in your left ear is to your left, a tone in both ears is ahead or behind, and a quiet one is far away. When a firefly is close enough to catch, a soft hum plays. That is "in reach". H names the way to the nearest glow in words, such as "5 metres ahead and to the left", and plays its tone from where it is.

## Catching a firefly

Space catches a firefly in reach, but only while it glows. Press as you hear its tone. A soft puff means it is dark just now: wait for its next glow and try again. Some species flash two or three times in a row. For those, press Space once for every flash of one blink. A light tick means one flash is answered. If the blink ends first, a falling sigh plays and you try its next blink. Nothing is lost.

## The jar and chords

The jar holds 6 fireflies. Tab sets them free. They fly up with a rising shimmer, the meadow glitters, and for a few seconds every firefly blinks more often. Each species rings one note of a five note scale: C, D, E, G or A. The notes of the jar ring together as a chord. Six chords have names, such as the Summer Triad, which is C, E and G. The first time you ring one, it goes in your journal.

## Species and the journal

There are 10 species. Each blinks its own pattern and rings its own note. The Dipper gives one long, low flash. The Twin Spark flashes twice, and the Triple Twinkle three times, high and quick. Others live only in one meadow or one season. The first time you catch a species, a rising chime plays and it goes in your journal. Journal, on the title, plays each species' blink and reads about it, and lists your chords and keepsakes. An unknown firefly's row says where to look for it.

## Meadows

The home meadow is open from the start. Catching fireflies opens the orchard, at 8 caught in all, the creek bank at 20, and the hayfield at 35. Each has its own species, landmarks and size. On the title, the Meadow row chooses which one the Play row walks. Practice catch is a small patch with one easy Dipper just ahead, in summer whatever the date, and nothing there counts.

## The seasons

The meadow follows the real season. Summer, from June to August, is full of fireflies. Spring, from March to May, has only the first few, and the frogs sing. In September, the last two fireflies of the year are out. In October and November, crickets sing, leaves skitter past, and one rare late firefly comes out if you wait.

## Midsummer night

Midsummer night opens on midsummer, the June solstice, from the day before to the day after. Until then its row on the title is locked and says how many days are left. On the night, you sit by a crackling bonfire while the whole meadow flashes together, 6 flashes in a row, then a rest. Some fireflies drift out of step and flash a moment late. Press Space with each chorus flash to bring one straggler back into step. Left and Right say how many are out of step on each side. After 12 rounds, you take home the year's keepsake, a different one each year, with how in step the meadow was.

## Fireworks

On the Fourth of July weekend, a summer evening has distant fireworks far off on the horizon: a soft boom and crackle now and then. That is July 3 to 5, and any Saturday or Sunday from July 1 to July 7.

## Status and hints

S gives the status, always in this order: in a challenge the time left, then your jar, and the nearest glow or "a firefly in reach". On a winter walk it gives the memories found instead of the jar. Press S again straight away for the fireflies caught tonight, their species, the time so far and the meadow. R repeats the last thing said. H names the way to the nearest glow and plays its tone.

## Records

Records keep the fireflies you have caught in all, species and chords found, your longest evening, evenings walked, jars set free, and your best midsummer night. Records, on the title, reads them with your challenge results.

## Challenges

Every player gets the same challenges, and they are always summer evenings, whatever the season outside. The daily is 4 minutes in one of the meadows. The weekly is 6 minutes with a small jar of 4, so chords come quickly. The monthly is 8 minutes in the hayfield. Each scores 10 points a firefly, 25 a species, and 40 for each named chord you set free. A voice warns you 30 seconds before the light fades. Heading home with Enter also ends it. Leave the challenge, on the pause menu, does not count, so you can try again.

## Sounds

Each firefly species has a soft tone of its own. A soft hum means a firefly is in reach. A glassy clink means it is in your jar, and a rising chime means a new species. A soft puff means it is dark, a light tick that a flash is answered, and a falling sigh that a blink ended. A tap on glass means the jar is full, and a dull hush that nothing is in reach. Crickets, frogs, the creek and the wind fill the evening. The sound check on the title plays each one with its name.

## Controls

Up, Down, Left and Right: walk one step. Up is north.

Space: catch a firefly as it glows, or remember a summer memory in winter.

Tab: set the jar free. In winter, look up at the stars.

S: status. Press it twice for the rest.

R: repeat the last thing said.

H: the way to the nearest glow.

Enter: head home and end the evening. It asks first: press Enter again.

F1: help for what you are doing right now. F2 stops speech.

Escape: pause, with Resume, Settings, Help, Back to the title, and Back to the lobby. In a challenge, Back to the title is Leave the challenge.

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's spoken instructions follow your bindings.
