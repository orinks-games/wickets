# Sonar Dive

## About the game

Salvage sub under a boat's bell. Ping for treasure, collect it, return to the bell to bank before air runs out. Each dive after the first goes deeper: the water is darker, the mines thicker, the currents stronger and the air shorter, but the treasure is worth more.

This help gives distances and depths in metres. Set Units to US in Settings and the game says feet instead.

Your score is the treasure you bank. An expedition ends when you choose to stop at the boat, when your air runs out, or when mines breach your hull. If you are lost at sea, the treasure aboard is lost with you, but everything you banked is kept.

## Hearing the sea

You hear the sea from the sub, facing where the sub faces. A sound straight ahead is ahead of you. One on your right is to your right. One behind you is a little muffled. When you turn, the world turns around you.

Ping: everything in range answers from where it is. Echo delay is distance—about half a second at 25 m, a full second at 50 m (there and back). Later and quieter means farther.

Directions in speech use a clock face: 12 o'clock is straight ahead, 3 o'clock to your right, 6 o'clock behind you and 9 o'clock to your left.

Turn on Spoken call-outs in Settings, from the title or the pause menu, to hear each ping's four nearest echoes named, with distance and clock direction. The hint key always names the nearest treasure.

## The echoes

A wreck is a hollow metal clang: a sunken ship with treasure in its hold. An empty wreck, one you have salvaged, is a dull thud.

A treasure chest is a bright double chime.

A mine is a harsh buzzing ring.

A rock is a short stony knock: an outcrop that will stop you. The rock wall at the edge of the dive site is a deep rumble.

A whale is a soft low hum. A current is a rushing hiss.

The galleon is a great gong, only in the monthly event. A glow pearl is a glassy rising chime, only in the glow dive.

Sound check, on the title, plays each of these with its name, and a demonstration of the same echo from five places around you.

## Treasure

Swim into a treasure chest to take it: its coins are aboard at once.

A wreck holds more, but takes work. Stop within a few metres of it, at all stop, and hold still while the grab arm clanks inside. Salvage takes 3 seconds. Moving off, or being pushed by a current, interrupts it, and you start again.

Treasure is worth more the deeper you go: a quarter more for every dive past the first.

## Dangers

Mines tick within about 16 m, faster as you close. Touch one: lose a hull point (start with 3; zero ends the expedition). Surfacing repairs one.

Rocks and the wall around the site scrape you and cost 3 seconds of air. Driving into them stops your engine: turn away and throttle up again. If a current pins you against rock, point away from it and throttle up. The engine keeps running while you pull clear.

Currents push you while you are inside them, and a rushing sound plays until you are out. Fight them at full ahead, or let them carry you. You cannot salvage inside a current.

## Air

Your tank holds 150 seconds on the first dive and less on each deeper dive. Full ahead uses air faster than slow ahead, and all stop uses the least. You are warned at half, at a quarter, and at 15 seconds. Press H at any time: when your air is short of what the trip home needs, it says so.

A friendly whale circles somewhere on most sites, singing as it goes. Pass within a few metres of it and it shares a breath of air: 25 seconds more, once a dive. It keeps its gift while your tank is still nearly full, so you can come back to it later.

## Buoys and the bell

The boat's bell rings every 5 seconds from where you began. Come back within a few metres of it to surface.

Tab drops a marker buoy that beeps every few seconds from where you left it. Up to 3 can be out at once, and a fourth takes the place of the oldest. Use them to mark a wreck you mean to come back to, or a safe path home past the mines.

## At the boat

When you surface, what you carry is banked, your air is refilled, and you choose: dive again, deeper, or end the expedition and keep your haul. Escape there opens the pause menu.

## After an expedition

When an expedition ends, you hear how it went, your haul and your best. Then the results menu offers Play again, or Try again after a loss, Hear the results again, and Back to the title. After a challenge, the last row is Back to the challenges. Escape does the same as the last row.

## Modes

- Play, expedition: from dive 1, as deep as you dare. Your best haul is on the row and in Records.
- Deep start: locked until an expedition reaches dive 4. Then it begins at dive 4, with its own best haul.
- Practice dive: endless air and harmless mines, to learn the sonar. Not scored.

Records, on the title, reads both best hauls, your deepest dive and your challenge results.

## Challenges

The challenges are the same sea for every player, and a new one comes each day, week and month. The Challenges menu shows each one's best result and your streak. Before each run you hear its rule and when the next one arrives. Afterwards you hear your result, your best and your streak. A run counts when it ends at the boat or at sea. Leave the challenge, on the pause menu, does not count, and neither does Escape on the briefing, which goes back without starting.

- Daily, Sprint dive: one dive, from dive 1 to 3, with a twist announced up front: murky water that halves your sonar, rich wrecks with more mines, rip currents, or thin air.
- Weekly, Long haul: up to 3 dives from dive 3, with no hull repairs between them, and a twist of its own.
- Monthly, The sunken galleon: on each dive, a galleon holding 1000 coins lies near the edge of the site, ringed by 6 mines. 2 dives from dive 4, and a single hull point. Salvaging the galleon takes 6 seconds of stillness.

## Special nights

- Moon pearl dive, on every full moon night. The title has a row for it. It is an expedition from dive 1 on a sea made from that full moon's date, so every player dives the same sea that night. Each dive also hides one moon pearl, worth 150 coins on the first dive and more the deeper you go. Its echo is a glassy rising chime, and you swim into it to collect it, like a chest. The moon pearl dive keeps its own best haul. Once the full moon has set, Play again says so and takes you back to the title.
- The whale road, on the spring and autumn equinoxes, from the day before to the day after. On every dive but the challenges, deep whale song carries now and then from far to the north, beyond the dive site. It is lower and longer than the friendly whale's call. It is only a landmark: there is nothing to find there and it changes nothing else. A ping stops it at once, and it never starts while a ping's echoes are on their way back. The first dive names it, and S and H mention it.

Sound check plays the glow pearl's echo, its pick up and the whale road too.

## Controls

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's spoken instructions follow your bindings.

- Left and Right arrows: turn while held. A soft click marks every 30 degrees, and your heading is spoken when you let go.
- Up and Down arrows: move the engine telegraph one step: slow astern, all stop, slow ahead, half ahead, full ahead.
- Space: send a sonar ping.
- Tab: drop a marker buoy where you are.
- S: air, hull, any current, and where the bell is. Press it again straight away for heading, throttle, coins aboard and banked, and the dive and its depth.
- R: repeat the last message.
- H: a hint, naming the nearest treasure and how to take it, any mine close by, and a warning when it is time to head home.
- Escape: pause. The pause menu can resume, open settings or this help, go back to the title, or go back to the lobby. During a challenge, its leave row is Leave the challenge.
- F1: help for the screen you are on. F2 stops speech.
