# Tide Pools

## About the game

The tide is out. A strip of rock pools lies along the shore, with the sea beyond them, ahead of you. Gulls call overhead and the wind comes and goes. Walk from pool to pool, lean in and listen, and gather the creatures you hear for your home aquarium. There is no way to fail: each trip ends when the tide comes back in.

## Walking the shore

Left and Right arrows walk along the rocks from one pool to the next. Each step splashes on wet rock. A soft thud into sand means the rocks end and there are no more pools that way.

You always face the sea, so the pools run from your left to your right. A sound on your left comes from a pool to your left.

## Leaning in and stepping back

Up brings you closer to the water and Down steps back. There are three places to stand:

- Standing back on the rocks: you hear the pool in front of you and two pools either side, all a little faint. Good for finding where things are.
- At the water's edge: you hear the pool in front of you and the next pool either side. Walking always brings you to the edge.
- Leaning in close: you hear only the pool in front of you, clearly, and you can reach in.

Leaning in over a pool with nothing left in it, you are told there is only clear water and weed.

## Gathering

Lean in close, listen, and press Space just after you hear something. You have about three seconds after a sound to reach for it.

If you press Space when you have not just heard anything, your hands come up with only water and you hear it drip. If you are not leaning in, you pat the rock instead: lean in with Up first.

The first time on a trip that you hear a creature in your pool, its name is spoken. Tab names the last sound you heard in your pool at any time.

## Shy creatures

Some creatures are shy: the shore crab, the goby, the hermit crab, the rock shrimp, the seahorse and the baby octopus. As you lean in, they dart out of sight with a quick falling swish and a plop.

Keep still. When you hear two soft rising notes, something has crept back out; listen for its sound and gather it. If you reach in too soon, it tucks itself away again and you must wait a little longer. Stepping back and leaning in again startles them all over again.

Shy creatures come out quickly at Sandy Cove and take longer on the Far Reef.

## The creatures

Common creatures, and their sounds:

- Shore crab: a quick skitter of clicking claws. Shy. 15 points.
- Periwinkle shell: a little shell rolling and clattering over pebbles. 5 points.
- Starfish: a slow, soft two-note twinkle. 10 points.
- Sea anemone: a soft, low, wet pop. 5 points.
- Little goby fish: a bright little splash. Shy. 20 points.
- Hermit crab: three wooden taps of a borrowed shell on rock. Shy. 15 points.
- Rock shrimp: two sharp snaps. Shy. 15 points.
- Mussel: a string of small bubbles. 5 points.

Rare creatures, 50 points each, only turn up under the right conditions:

- Cowrie shell, on a spring tide: a glossy shell rolling with a glassy ring.
- Seahorse, on a sunny day: a tiny, breathy rising whistle. Shy.
- Baby octopus, at dusk: a low gurgle and a squirt of ink. Shy.
- Moon jellyfish, when it is breezy: a slow, soft pulse.
- Sand dollar, in fog: a hollow tap and two low, round notes.
- Sea slug, in drizzle: a slow, wobbling squelch.

Sound check, on the title menu, plays every one with its name.

## Weather, tides and time of day

Every trip has its own weather, tide and time of day, told to you before you set off, with any rare creature the day might bring. The weather is sunny, breezy, foggy or drizzly. A neap tide is small and the trip is shorter; a spring tide is the lowest of the month and the trip is longer. The weather changes the beach around you too: more gulls on sunny days, gusts when it is breezy, a distant foghorn in fog, soft rain in drizzle.

## The tide

A distant bell buoy rings once when the tide turns, halfway through the trip, twice when there is about a minute left, and twice again at 20 seconds. The waves ahead grow louder and nearer as the water comes back. When a big wave washes over the rocks, the trip is over and you head home. A trip also ends early if you gather everything on the shore.

## After a trip

When a trip ends, you hear what you gathered, your score and what is new in your aquarium, all on one page. Then a short menu offers Play again, Hear the results again, Your aquarium, and Back to the title. After a challenge, Play again becomes Play this challenge again, and the last row is Back to the challenges. Escape does the same as the last row.

## Your aquarium

Everything you gather goes home to your aquarium, which keeps growing. Leaving a trip early from the pause menu still brings home what you gathered, though the shore's best score waits for a finished trip. Visit it from Your aquarium on the title menu: each kind lives in its own spot from left to right, and they make their sounds now and then, more often as the tank fills.

In the aquarium, Left and Right move along the tanks and say each kind's name, how many you have and where you first found one. Space listens to one again, Tab plays the whole tank from left to right, S sums it up, and H tells you a kind you are still missing and where to look.

## Stretches of shore

- Sandy Cove: 6 pools and about 3 minutes, gentle creatures, quick to come out.
- Rocky Point: 9 pools and about 4 minutes, with gobies and hermit crabs and better odds of a rare find. Opens when your aquarium holds 4 kinds.
- The Far Reef: 12 pools and about 5 minutes, every common creature, the shyest ones, and the best odds of a rare find. Opens at 7 kinds.

Each find scores its points, and each place keeps your best score.

The Play row on the title menu starts a trip. Left and Right on it choose the shore, and it reads the shore's best score. A shore you have not opened yet reads as locked, and Enter on it says how many kinds open it. Records, on the title menu, reads every best score and your challenge results.

## Practice pools

Three sheltered pools close to home, with no tide: an anemone, a shy shore crab, and a periwinkle shell with a starfish. Gather all four to finish. A good place to learn leaning in, waiting and gathering.

## Challenges

Challenges give every player the same shore for a day, a week or a month, with a gentle goal. Each finished trip counts toward your streak. Leave the challenge, on the pause menu, does not count, so you can come back and try again.

- Daily: tonight's three species. A short dusk trip on Rocky Point with three species to find, each worth 100 points more, and one twist. The twist is still water, where shy creatures come out twice as fast; a busy shore, where every pool holds something; a quick tide, a shorter trip where every find is worth double; or hushed, where you hear only the pool in front of you.
- Weekly: this week's rare find. A long trip on the Far Reef, where one rare creature is waiting somewhere. Finding it is worth 300 points.
- Monthly: the Great Low Tide. 15 pools on a spring tide, when every rare creature can turn up whatever the weather, and every different kind you gather is worth 40 points more. Gathering 8 different kinds meets the goal.

The challenge list says your best and streak for each, and how long until the next one.

## Calendar events

Some days the shore is different. Each event has a row of its own on the title menu, just below Play. While it is closed, the row says locked and tells you when it opens. It takes you to the furthest shore you have opened, and keeps its own best score. If the event has ended by the time you choose Play again, you hear that it has ended and go back to the title. The ordinary shores and the challenges never change.

- King tide: within a day of a new or full moon, from November to February. The sea goes far out, so two extra pools open at each end of the shore, on a spring tide with a longer trip. The waves start far off and quiet, and curlews call from the wet sand. This is the game's take on the big winter tides, not a real tide table.
- Moonlit pools: on every full moon night, all year. A night trip under the full moon. The gulls are asleep, and a night heron croaks along the shore. Moon jellyfish and baby octopuses can come out whatever the weather.

The first curlew and the first heron of a trip are named when you hear them. S says which event you are on. Sound check plays both birds.

## Controls

- Left and Right: walk between pools.
- Up: lean closer. Down: step back.
- Space: reach in and gather what you just heard.
- Tab: name the last sound you heard in your pool.
- S: which pool you are at and how long until the tide returns. Press it again straight away for what you have gathered and your score, and a third time for the shore and its weather.
- H: a hint: what to do at this pool, or which way the nearest creature is.
- R: repeat the last message.
- F1: help for where you are right now.
- Escape: pause, with Resume, Settings, Help, Back to the title and Back to the lobby. During a challenge, Back to the title is Leave the challenge.

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's spoken instructions follow your bindings.
