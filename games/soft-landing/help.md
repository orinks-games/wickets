## About the game

You are piloting a small survey lander down to a landing pad on a distant moon. Gravity pulls you toward the surface, and holding the main engine fires your thrusters to slow your fall. Fuel is tight, so the trick is to fall freely at first and save your burn for the final approach. Touch down below the safe speed to land smoothly, or hit too hard and crash.

This help uses metric heights and speeds. If you switch Units to US in Settings, the game reports feet and feet a second instead, with call-out marks every 300, 150, or 30 feet.

## Starting a descent

Every flight begins with the lander hovering high above the pad. Mission control reads out the moon, its gravity, your starting height, and the safe landing speed. Press Space to begin the descent, then let go so the engine fires on your next press.

## The engine and fuel

Hold Space to fire the main engine, and let go to fall freely. Firing slows your descent and burns fuel, measured in seconds of burn time. Warnings sound at 25 percent and 10 percent fuel, and the engine cuts out once the tank runs dry.

## Safe speed

Keep your falling speed below the moon's safe speed before you touch the pad. Mission control calls Too fast when you cross above the limit and Safe speed when you slow back under it. Near the pad, hold the engine until the call confirms Safe speed.

## The pad beacon

The pad beacon helps you judge how close the ground is getting. Sound check on the title plays the beacon along with the other gameplay cues.

## Call-outs

Mission control calls your altitude as you pass each mark, along with your falling speed in metres a second. Marks start at every 50 metres. Up arrow spaces call-outs farther apart and Down arrow makes them more frequent, cycling between 100, 50, and 10 metres. Your choice is saved everywhere except the practice pad. Tab requests an immediate call-out with height, speed, and fuel, and R repeats the last call-out.

## Status and hints

S reads your height, falling speed in metres a second, and remaining fuel. Press S again right away for the moon, its safe speed, and a hint. The pad is always straight below you. H calculates what to do next, such as falling for about 10 seconds before burning, or burning now for about 3 seconds. Hints never recommend a burn longer than the fuel you have left.

## The moons

The survey covers three moons, unlocked one after another as you land on each one.

Moon 1, Thistle: light gravity, 300 metres up, safe speed 3 metres a second.

Moon 2, Marrow: medium gravity, 450 metres up, safe speed 3 metres a second, with a faster starting drop.

Moon 3, Ember: heavy gravity, 600 metres up, safe speed 2.5 metres a second, and a tighter fuel tank.

Moon select flies any moon you have already unlocked.

## Pace

The lander pauses in place while a call-out or spoken line finishes. If you are holding the engine and burning fuel, the lander keeps flying through speech. Asking for the same line over and over stops pausing the descent after a while. This works the same way at every pace and in every mode.

Pace in Settings controls how fast the descent runs: relaxed, normal, or quick. Normal is the default and sits slightly slower than quick. Quick is the fastest, while relaxed runs at half speed. Your fall, engine thrust, fuel burn, and flight timer all follow your chosen pace.

Changing pace takes effect on your next flight. Challenges and the practice pad keep the pace they started on until you leave them.

Relaxed scores and your softest relaxed touchdown are saved separately, including for challenges and events. Normal and quick share their scores. Moons you unlock count at every pace.

## Scoring and records

A safe landing earns 200 points, remaining fuel adds up to 300 points for a full tank, and a soft touchdown adds up to 500 points depending on how slowly you touch down. A crash scores nothing.

Records tracks your best score on each moon, your softest touchdown, your total landings and crashes, and your challenge results.

## The tutorial

The tutorial walks you through four short lessons on the practice pad with endless fuel. Lesson 1 covers falling freely, lesson 2 covers braking with the engine down to Safe speed, lesson 3 covers asking for a hint, and lesson 4 guides you through a full landing by calling Burn now and Let go. If you crash, it tells you what to adjust and resets you at the top.

## The practice pad

The practice pad flies the first moon with endless fuel and call-outs every 10 metres once you slow down. Only your count of practice landings is saved, and each touchdown resets you at the top for another run.

## Challenges

Every player flies the same seeded moons for each period. A crash ends the run, though any landings you already completed still count.

Today's landing is the daily challenge: one seeded moon with a tank holding twice the best burn. A new landing arrives every day.

The weekly survey is the weekly challenge: three moons in a row sharing one fuel tank. Fuel you spend on an earlier moon is gone for the next, and the fuel bonus is awarded once at the end. A new survey arrives every Monday.

The deep moon is the monthly challenge: one heavy-gravity moon with very little spare fuel, a safe speed of 2.5 metres a second, and no hints. A new moon arrives on the first of every month.

Choosing Leave the challenge from the pause menu stops the run without counting it, so you can come back and try again later.

## Special nights

- Full moon pad opens on every full moon night with its own row on the title. It builds a single moon from that full moon's date so every player flies the same descent, named for the full moon such as Harvest Moon or Hunter's Moon, with a tank holding twice the best burn.
- Tranquility pad opens from 19 to 21 July, around the anniversary of the first moon landing, with its own row on the title. You land at Tranquility Base in light gravity from 400 metres up with a roomy tank, and mission control confirms that the Eagle has landed when you touch down safely.

Each special night keeps its own best score without unlocking survey moons or changing your softest touchdown. Once its window closes, Play again lets you know and returns you to the title.

## Pausing

Escape pauses the flight and holds the lander in place until you resume, when mission control reads your height and speed. If you were holding Space when you paused, release it and press again before the engine fires.

## Controls

Space: before the descent, start the flight. During the descent, hold to fire the main engine.

Up arrow: space altitude call-outs farther apart.

Down arrow: make altitude call-outs more frequent.

Left arrow and Right arrow: not used, since this lander has no side thrusters.

Tab: request an immediate call-out from mission control.

S: status, reading height, speed, and fuel. Press S again right away for the moon, safe speed, and a hint.

R: repeat the last call-out.

H: ask for a hint on when to burn. Not available in the monthly challenge.

F1: help for what you are doing right now. F2 stops speech.

Escape: pause menu, with Resume, Settings, Help, Back to the title, and Back to the lobby. In a challenge, Back to the title becomes Leave the challenge.

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's instructions follow your bindings.
