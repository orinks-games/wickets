## About the game

Larkfield starts as a quiet, empty hamlet beside a river, and you are its first farmer. Work the fields, fish from the dock, chop wood and raise buildings along the green as newcomers arrive at the gate asking to stay. Every settler brings a past trade, two traits and a three-step dream to fulfill. Keep everyone fed and cheerful and the hamlet thrives; on steady and lively paces, dangers can cost crops, a hen or repairs, and a settler left glum too long may move on, though nobody ever dies.

## Walking the lane

Larkfield runs along one lane. From left to right you pass the gate, the cottages, the green, the fields, the yard, the river and the woods. Left and Right walk along the lane, Up steps into the place beside you, and Down steps back out to the lane. Inside a place, Left and Right move between its spots, such as the hen house and the goat pen by the cottages, and every step names where you are.

Everything plays from its position relative to where you stand. Sound check on the title menu plays the footsteps, fishing cues, dangers and settlers' instruments.

## The fields

The fields are three rows of four plots. Up moves a row deeper into the fields, and Down steps back toward the lane. On any plot, Space does whatever it needs next: dig rough ground, sow a dug plot, water a growing crop or harvest a ripe one. Tab pulls weeds.

Each night, a watered crop with no weeds grows by one day, and rain waters every plot for you. Crops sown outside their season grow more slowly. Finished crops hold back a seed for next time, and crops like peas and strawberries keep producing without being resown. Once you build the well, settlers water the fields too.

## Settlers

Newcomers arrive at the gate asking to join the hamlet. Walk to the gate and press Enter to meet them, then decide whether to welcome them or send them on. Every settler had a past trade, such as baking or sailing, and works fastest at that job. Each also has two traits, like early riser or sweet tooth, that shape how they work and what lifts their spirits.

Settlers pick their own work from farming, cooking, building, crafting, animals, fishing, mending or music. Press J to open the job board, where Left and Right set a settler's first-choice job; when none of that work is left, they pitch in somewhere else.

Press Enter to chat with whoever is at your spot. A friendly word cheers them up, and they share what they need. Press P to read through everyone in Larkfield, including their traits, best work, mood and what is on their mind.

## Moods and wobbles

Every settler is glum, fine or cheerful depending on food, company, cottage comfort and the day's news. Cheerful settlers work faster, while glum ones slow down.

If a settler stays glum for a few days, they hit a harmless wobble: sulking on the bridge, baking scones nobody asked for, playing the same tune over and over, or slipping off to the next village for the night. Find them and press Enter to talk them round; anyone who went to the next village returns the next morning.

On steady and lively paces, a settler who stays glum much longer may decide to leave for good. They warn you twice in the morning news first, and S and H point out who needs help, so talk to them and see to what they want in time. On the gentle pace, nobody ever leaves.

## Dreams and keepsakes

Every settler carries a three-step dream, such as a proper oven or a boat on the river. Each step calls for something specific: a building, stocked goods in the larder, enough neighbours, a festival or a cheerful day. Completed steps appear in the bedtime news, and finishing all three earns a keepsake on your shelf, which you can browse from the title menu.

## Buildings

Eight buildings can be raised on the green and in the yard. Walk to an open spot and press Space to check what belongs there and what it costs in wood, stone and coins. Starting a project spends the materials right away, and settlers on building jobs put it up while you can press Space at the site to hammer alongside them. One building goes up at a time.

The mill grinds grain into flour, and the bakery turns it into bread or fruit pies. The smokehouse preserves fish, the workshop crafts furniture for the cottages, and the dairy makes cheese from goat's milk. The well waters the fields, the inn draws visitors, and the bandstand brings music to the green after supper.

Gather wood from fallen trees in the woods and stone from the old quarry by pressing Space to swing your axe or pick. Every third axe swing yields 1 wood, and every third pick swing yields 1 stone.

## The barge

The river barge docks every third day, and pressing Space at the dock opens trade. You can sell goods from the larder and buy seeds, nails, hens and a goat. Buying seeds for the current season is the cheapest way to keep the fields full.

## Food and supper

When the supper bell rings at dusk, everyone eats from the larder. A meal with two or more good foods lifts everyone's spirits, while a thin supper lowers moods and prompts the next village to send over bread. Pick who to sit with to brighten their evening, hear the day's news, and head to bed.

Press S anytime to check how many days of food are in the larder. Fish at the river, forage in the woods, collect eggs and harvest the fields to keep it stocked.

## Fishing

At the river's fishing spot, press Space to cast your line. Wait for a bite, then press Space straight away to reel the fish in before it slips off.

## The almanac and troubles

Each morning brings fresh news: the weather, someone waiting at the gate, a passing visitor or a small trouble. A trouble stalls one part of the hamlet until it is fixed. Crows in the fields halt crop growth until you press Tab there to scare them off, and a leaking roof stops a building until you press Space there to mend it. Settlers on building jobs also handle troubles, and H always points you to the first one.

Pace in Settings controls how lively the almanac is. Gentle brings few troubles, easygoing moods and no dangers. Lively brings more visitors, more dangers and stormier weather.

## Dangers

On steady and lively paces, your hamlet faces occasional dangers. Each costs something if ignored, though anything lost can always be rebuilt or replaced. S and H always put an active danger first.

A fire can break out at a building. Press Space there several times to throw buckets of water, and settlers on building jobs will help. Left too long, the fire burns through and stops the building until you press Space there with 3 wood to repair it.

A fox can sneak up to the hen house by the cottages. Press Space or Tab there quickly to chase it off before it makes off with a hen.

After spring or autumn rain, the river can swell. Press Space at the dock to pile 2 stone along the bank; if you leave it until supper, the far row of fields washes out and has to be sown again.

At dusk, a settler working in the woods can lose their way. Head into the woods and press Enter to find them before supper, or they stumble home cold and glum.

In winter, a hard frost can settle in for a few days and make everyone eat more at supper, so keep the larder well stocked.

## Seasons and festivals

A year has four ten-day seasons: spring, summer, autumn and winter. Each season has its own crops, and the barge stocks its seeds. Day ten of every season is a festival: sowing day, midsummer, the harvest fair and the lantern walk. Supper becomes a feast, everyone's spirits rise, and word spreads so a newcomer arrives the next morning.

## The hamlet's tune

Every settler plays their own instrument in the hamlet's tune, assigned in the order they arrived. Their music drifts from wherever they are working and shifts with their mood. Sound check on the title menu plays each settler's instrument.

## Challenges

Three shared challenges give every player the exact same small hamlet and a handful of days to hit a goal.

Market day, the daily challenge: fill the barge's order within five days. Food set aside for the barge is never eaten at supper.

Good spirits, the weekly challenge: welcome three tired newcomers and get all three cheerful at the same supper within six days.

Founders, the monthly challenge: build the mill and the bakery, then hold the season's festival supper on day ten.

Your score counts what is in the larder, how many settlers are cheerful, what you built, completing the goal and any days to spare.

## Barge fair and music night

On the third Tuesday of every month, a barge fair brings the barge every single day and pays an extra coin for everything you sell. On the fourth Sunday of every month, music night brings a band from the next village to play on the green after supper. Both events happen in your own hamlet and never affect challenges.

## Settings

Pace sets how busy the almanac is, whether dangers occur and whether glum settlers can leave. Day length sets how much time you have between morning and the supper bell. You can also toggle the settlers' tune off to leave just the birds and the river, and toggle off work from along the lane so only work at your current spot plays.

## Controls

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's instructions follow your bindings.

- Left and Right: walk the lane or move between spots inside a place.
- Up: step into a place or move a row deeper into the fields.
- Down: step back out toward the lane.
- Space: do the job in front of you, such as digging, sowing, watering, harvesting, chopping, fishing, building or trading.
- Tab: pull weeds, or feed the hens or the goat.
- Enter: talk to whoever is where you stand.
- J: open the job board.
- L: list what is around you.
- P: browse everyone in Larkfield, one page per settler.
- S: check what needs attention and how many days of food you have. Press S again straight away for the day and stores, and a third time for everyone's mood and work.
- H: suggest what to do next.
- R: repeat the last line.
- Escape: pause the game.
