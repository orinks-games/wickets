# Slow Kettle

## About the game

Keep a small tea house at your own pace. Guests name a taste; you pick tea and cup, heat by ear, pour, and lift the leaves when the steep is right. No fail: every cup pleases someone; a perfect one earns a note and a tip.

## A guest comes in

A small brass door bell rings, then soft footsteps come to the counter, and the guest says what they would like, such as "something smoky and strong" or "a calm cup before bed". That is their taste. A guest might want something calm, bright, strong, smoky, floral, warming, sweet or fresh. Any key hurries their walk to the counter. R says their words again.

## The shelf and your cups

Up and Down move along your shelf. Each tea is named, with a short description. Left and Right choose a cup from your cupboard. Tab tells you more about the tea: the tastes it suits, the heat it wants, and how long it likes to steep. Space puts the tea in the pot. Up or Down before the kettle is on takes you back to the shelf.

Each cup suits some tastes: the big mug for strong and warming cups, the clay cup for calm and smoky ones, the glass for fresh and bright ones, the thin porcelain for floral and sweet ones. A cup that suits the taste makes the guest a little happier. The plain white cup is good for anything.

## The kettle

Space puts the kettle on, with a click. Listen to its song as it heats: a low rumble from about 40 degrees, small bubbles from about 65, a soft hiss from about 80, a rolling hiss from about 92, and at 100 degrees, the boil, with a gentle whistle. From cold to the boil takes about 13 seconds. Space again takes the kettle off and pours onto the leaves.

Each tea wants its own heat. Green and white teas want water well before the boil, around 75 or 80 degrees: pour during the small bubbles or as the hiss starts. Oolongs and roasted teas want about 90. Black teas and herbs want the boil: wait for the whistle. S says the kettle's heat and the heat the tea wants, and Tab lets you listen closely.

## Steeping

Once the water is poured, a soft clock ticks once a second while the leaves steep. Space lifts the leaves out when it tastes right. Each tea likes its own steep, from 5 to 15 seconds: green teas short, herbs long. Tab lets you breathe in the steam, and says whether it is still pale, taking colour, rich and ready, getting strong or very strong. S says the seconds so far. After 40 seconds the leaves come out on their own.

## How the cup went

The guest drinks and says how it was: perfect, lovely, pleasant or kind. Every cup scores up to 100. A tea that suits the guest's taste is worth the most, then water at the right heat, within 3 degrees, and the right steep, within 1 second. A cup that suits the taste adds a little. A perfect cup needs 90. When a cup is not perfect, the guest says the one thing that would help most, such as "A little strong" or "It wanted hotter water."

## Coins and tips

Every cup pays 2 coins. A lovely cup pays 3. A perfect cup pays 2 and leaves a tip of 3, with a kind note on the saucer. A pot poured for two pays twice. Coins are kept between visits. S says the coins earned so far.

## The shop

The shop is on the title, and on the results after each afternoon as Visit the shop. It sells 11 more teas, 4 more cups, a music box with three tunes, and a sand timer. A new tea goes on your shelf, a new cup in your cupboard. The music box plays its tune quietly in the tea house; the Music box row in Settings changes the tune or turns it off, from the title or the pause menu. The sand timer chimes softly when a steep reaches what its tea wants.

## Regulars and their stories

Some guests are regulars. Each visit they tell you the next part of their story: a retired postman walking his old round, a nurse and her balcony tomato plant, a cello student and her dog, a clockmaker mending the town clock, and more who come in later. The guest book on the title keeps every part you have heard. A regular never comes in two afternoons running if another is waiting their turn.

## Seasons and holidays

Your shelf has a blend for each season, free all season: Spring meadow from March to May, Summer berry from June to August and Autumn hearth from September to November.

On certain holidays a special guest comes in at the end of the afternoon, with a special brew on your shelf for that day only. Lunar New Year brings Mei with tangerine oolong. Valentine's Day brings an old couple for rose tea for two, one pot poured into two cups. The spring equinox brings cherry blossom tea, midsummer an iced mint tea, steeped short and poured over ice, and the autumn equinox roasted barley. On Halloween a cloaked stranger asks for a smoky lapsang, and pays the way Lantern Jack always pays. Thanksgiving brings spiced apple.

Each holiday guest leaves a keepsake, which the Keepsakes row on the title keeps, with its year. The day counts from the day before to the day after, so it falls right in every time zone. In the days before a holiday, a regular may mention it.

## Long evening

After 3 afternoons the long evening opens on the title: seven guests on a rainy evening, three of them regulars. It keeps its own best.

## Practice brewing

Practice brewing is a quiet place to learn the kettle. Brew any tea on your shelf for yourself, as often as you like. After each cup you hear the exact heat you poured at and the seconds you steeped, beside what the tea wants. No guests, no coins.

## Hints

H gives a hint for what you are doing: teas on your shelf that suit the guest's taste and a cup that suits it, when to pour by the kettle's song, or how many seconds the tea likes. Hints are free and as many as you like.

## Spoken call-outs

With spoken call-outs on, the kettle's song is also spoken as it changes, such as "Soft hiss, about 80 degrees", and the steep's seconds every 5 seconds. Turn them off in Settings, from the title or the pause menu, to brew by ear alone. Nothing changes in how a cup is judged.

## Challenges

Every player gets the same guests. The daily is 3 guests, the weekly 6, and the monthly is the tasting society: 5 members who each ask for a tea by name. In a challenge every year-round tea and every cup is yours, the sand timer stays at home, and no coins are earned. Each cup scores up to 100, and the scores are added. Your result counts once the last guest has their cup. Leave the challenge, on the pause menu, does not count, so you can come back later.

## Records

Records keep the afternoons and evenings you have played, cups served, perfect cups, coins earned, your best afternoon and best evening in coins, teas discovered out of every tea there is, keepsakes, and your challenge results.

## Sounds

A small brass bell and soft footsteps: a guest comes in. On Halloween a cold gust of wind comes in instead of footsteps. A wooden tick: moving along the shelf. A tin lid: the tea goes in the pot. A click: the kettle is on. Its song is a low rumble, a rushing hiss, and at the boil, a gentle whistle. A splash: water poured. A soft clock tick: one second of steeping. A drip and a swish: the leaves are lifted. Each cup has its own sound as it is set down. Three warm rising notes are a perfect cup, two a lovely one, one soft note a kind one. Coins land on the counter after each cup. The sound check on the title plays each one with its name.

## Controls

Up and Down: move along the shelf.

Left and Right: choose a cup.

Space: choose the tea, put the kettle on, pour, lift the leaves, pour each cup of a pot, then welcome the next guest.

Tab: more about the tea, listen to the kettle, or breathe in the steam.

S: status: the kettle, the steep or the cups, and while you choose, what the guest would like. Press S again straight away for which guest this is, what they would like and your coins.

R: hear the guest's words again.

H: a hint.

F1: help for what you are doing right now. F2 stops speech.

Escape: pause, with Resume, Settings, Help, Back to the title, and Back to the lobby. In a challenge, Back to the title is Leave the challenge.

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's spoken instructions follow your bindings.
