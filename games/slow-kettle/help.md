# Slow Kettle

## About the game

Run a small neighborhood tea house at your own unhurried pace. Guests stop by the counter and tell you what they are in the mood for, and you pick a tea from your shelf, choose a matching cup, heat the kettle to the right temperature, and time the steep. No cup ever fails, and every guest leaves glad they came. Nail the match, heat, and steep for a perfect cup, and you earn a tip and a thank-you note on the saucer.

## A guest comes in

A guest walks up to the counter and asks for a mood or taste, like "something smoky and strong" or "a calm cup before bed". Guests ask for calm, bright, strong, smoky, floral, warming, sweet, or fresh cups. Press any key to hurry their walk to the counter, and press R any time to repeat their request.

## The shelf and your cups

Up and Down move along your tea shelf, naming each tea with a short description. Left and Right choose a cup from your cupboard. Tab gives you more detail on the tea, including the tastes it suits, the water temperature it likes, and its steeping time. Space measures the tea into the pot, and Up or Down before the kettle goes on takes you back to the shelf.

Each cup pairs well with certain tastes: the big mug for strong and warming cups, the clay cup for calm and smoky ones, the glass for fresh and bright ones, and the thin porcelain for floral and sweet ones. Picking a matching cup makes the guest a little happier, while the plain white cup works fine for anything.

## The kettle

Space puts the kettle on, and it takes about 13 seconds to heat from cold to a full boil. Press Space again to lift the kettle off and pour onto the leaves.

Different teas want different water temperatures. Green and white teas like around 75 or 80 degrees, oolongs and roasted teas like about 90, and black teas and herbs want 100 degrees. S reads the current temperature alongside what the tea wants, and Tab checks the kettle up close.

## Steeping

Once you pour, the leaves start steeping, and Space lifts them out. Most teas steep best between 5 and 15 seconds, with green teas on the shorter side and herbs on the longer side. Tab checks whether the brew is still pale, taking colour, rich and ready, getting strong, or very strong. S gives the seconds elapsed so far. After 40 seconds, the leaves come out on their own.

## How the cup went

Your guest takes a sip and tells you how it turned out: perfect, lovely, pleasant, or kind. Every cup scores up to 100 points. Picking a tea that matches the guest's taste matters most, followed by water within 3 degrees of the target and a steep within 1 second. A matching cup adds a small bonus, and 90 or higher makes a perfect cup. Whenever a cup falls short of perfect, the guest mentions the one change that would help most, like "A little strong" or "It wanted hotter water."

## Coins and tips

Every cup pays at least 2 coins, and a lovely cup pays 3. A perfect cup pays 2 coins plus a 3-coin tip and a kind note on the saucer. A pot poured for two pays double. Your coins carry over from visit to visit, and S reports what you have earned so far.

## The shop

You can open the shop from the title, or from the results screen after an afternoon as Visit the shop. It stocks 11 more teas, 4 more cups, a music box with three tunes, and a sand timer. New teas appear on your shelf and new cups go into your cupboard.

The music box plays its tune in the background of the tea house, and the Music box row in Settings switches tunes or turns it off from the title or the pause menu. The sand timer chimes when your tea reaches its target steeping time.

## Regulars and their stories

Some visitors are regulars who drop in over multiple afternoons, sharing the next piece of their story each time. You will meet a retired postman walking his old round, a nurse tending her balcony tomato plant, a cello student and her dog, a clockmaker repairing the town clock, and others who arrive later. The guest book on the title saves every part you have heard, and a regular never visits two afternoons in a row while another regular is waiting.

## Seasons and holidays

Your shelf always includes a free seasonal blend: Spring meadow from March to May, Summer berry from June to August, and Autumn hearth from September to November.

On special holidays, a guest stops in at the end of the afternoon, and a special brew appears on your shelf for that day. Lunar New Year brings Mei for tangerine oolong. Valentine's Day brings an old couple sharing rose tea for two from one pot. The spring equinox brings cherry blossom tea, midsummer brings iced mint tea poured over ice, and the autumn equinox brings roasted barley. On Halloween a cloaked stranger asks for smoky lapsang and pays the way Lantern Jack always pays. Thanksgiving brings spiced apple.

Every holiday guest leaves behind a keepsake, stored with its year under Keepsakes on the title. Each holiday window runs from the day before through the day after so it lands cleanly in every time zone, and regulars sometimes mention an upcoming holiday beforehand.

## Long evening

After 3 afternoons, Long evening unlocks on the title: a rainy evening service with seven guests, including three regulars. It tracks its own best coin total.

## Practice brewing

Practice brewing gives you a calm counter with no guests and no coins. Brew any tea on your shelf as many times as you like, and after each cup the game reads back the exact temperature you poured at and how many seconds you steeped beside what the tea prefers.

## Hints

H gives a free hint for whatever step you are on: teas and cups on your shelf that suit the guest's taste, when to pour, or how many seconds the tea likes to steep. Use hints as often as you want.

## Spoken call-outs

With spoken call-outs turned on, the game announces kettle temperatures as they rise, such as "Soft hiss, about 80 degrees", and calls out the steep every 5 seconds. You can switch them off in Settings from the title or the pause menu without changing how any cup is judged.

## Challenges

Every player serves the same seeded line-up of guests. The daily challenge is 3 guests, the weekly is 6, and the monthly is the tasting society, where 5 members each ask for a specific tea by name.

In a challenge, every year-round tea and every cup is on hand, the sand timer stays put away, and no coins are earned. Each cup scores up to 100 and the scores add together. Your result counts once the last guest has been served. Choosing Leave the challenge from the pause menu does not count, so you can come back later.

## Records

Records tracks the afternoons and evenings you have worked, cups served, perfect cups, total coins earned, your best afternoon and best evening in coins, teas discovered out of every tea in the game, keepsakes collected, and your challenge results.

## Sound check

Sound check on the title plays the gameplay cues.

## Controls

Up and Down: move along the shelf.

Left and Right: choose a cup.

Space: pick the tea, put the kettle on, pour, lift the leaves, pour each cup of a pot, and welcome the next guest.

Tab: more detail on the tea, check the kettle up close, or check the steam.

S: status, reading the kettle, steep, or cups, or what the guest asked for while you choose. Press S again right away for which guest you are serving, their request, and your coins.

R: repeat the guest's request.

H: ask for a hint.

F1: help for what you are doing right now. F2 stops speech.

Escape: pause menu, with Resume, Settings, Help, Back to the title, and Back to the lobby. In a challenge, Back to the title becomes Leave the challenge.

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's instructions follow your bindings.
