# Stargazer

## About the game

Lie back on a dark hilltop and explore the real northern evening sky for the current season. Sweep your listening point across the night, trace constellations in order to add them and their lore to your star journal, and watch for hidden wonders, full moons, and meteor showers. There is no clock and no score to chase, so you can roam the sky at your own pace.

## The listening point

Left and Right sweep your listening point around the sky in 5-degree steps, while Up and Down raise and lower it from the horizon all the way to straight overhead.

You begin facing south, 40 degrees above the horizon. Whenever you cross due north, east, south, or west, the game announces the direction, and pressing S reports your exact position, such as "Southwest, 40 degrees up".

## Stars near the point

Stars within about 30 degrees of your listening point respond with cues for direction, height, and brightness. You can try all of these cues in Sound check from the title menu.

When your point rests directly on a star, the game names it, such as "Betelgeuse, in Orion". A star that is not part of any pattern tonight is called "a faint star", and those scatter differently each night.

## Tracing a constellation

Every constellation has its stars arranged in a set order, such as Orion: Betelgeuse, Bellatrix, Mintaka, Alnilam, Alnitak, Saiph, and then Rigel. When you land on the star that comes next, the game says "next", such as "Bellatrix, next in Orion". Press Space to trace it.

If you press Space on a star in the right constellation out of order, the game names the star you need next. Once you trace the final star, the constellation plays its own tune, and the first time you finish it, it goes into your star journal along with its lore.

## Hints and the guide tone

Press H to get the name of the next star to find and how many steps away it is, such as "Bellatrix, next in Orion: 3 steps right, 1 step up". Hints help you finish a constellation you have started, then point to ones missing from your journal, and then to the nearest one.

Tab toggles the guide tone on or off, leading you toward the next star and showing both its direction and how close you are getting.

## The sky and the calendar

The sky follows the real northern hemisphere evening calendar. Spring, from March to May, brings Leo, Bootes, Virgo, and Cancer. Summer, from June to August, brings Lyra, Cygnus, Aquila, Scorpius, and Sagittarius, with Lyra's Vega, Cygnus's Deneb, and Aquila's Altair forming the Summer Triangle. Autumn, from September to November, brings Pegasus, Andromeda, Perseus, and Aries.

Ursa Major, Ursa Minor, Cassiopeia, Cepheus, and Draco circle the pole star and stay up all year, shifting positions with each season.

## New constellations

You begin with the brightest few: the Big Dipper's Ursa Major, Cassiopeia, and one or two of the current season's constellations. Once your journal holds 2 constellations, more rise into view, and even more arrive at 5. New ones can rise right in the middle of a night, and the game announces them when they do. Come back across every season to fill your whole journal.

## Wonders

Each season hides a handful of deep-sky wonders: the Orion Nebula and the Pleiades in winter, the Beehive cluster in spring, the Ring Nebula and the heart of the Milky Way in summer, and the Andromeda Galaxy and the Double Cluster in autumn. Mizar and Alcor, in the Big Dipper's handle, are up all year round.

You can detect a wonder once your listening point comes within about 2 steps of it. Rest directly on it and press Space to log it.

## The full moon

On a full moon night, the moon rises low in the east. Rest your point on it and press Space to greet it, adding it to your journal under its traditional name, such as the Hunter's Moon in October. A second full moon in the same month is a blue moon, and under a full moon, faint stars are fewer and dimmer.

## Meteor showers

The year's major meteor showers arrive on their real peak nights: the Quadrantids on 3 January, the Lyrids on 22 April, the Eta Aquariids on 6 May, the Perseids on 12 August, the Orionids on 21 October, and the Leonids on 17 November. The peak runs from the day before to the day after, and for 3 days ahead of that, a few early shooting stars streak past.

On those nights, the Meteor shower row opens on the title menu. On other nights it stays locked, and its help names the next shower and how many days away it is.

## Making wishes

During a meteor watch you lie still under the sky. Press Space while a shooting star streaks past, or right after it fades, to make a wish. Fireballs move more slowly across the sky. Tab reads the shower's story, and pressing Enter twice heads home, tallying your wishes for that shower and year.

## Heading home

You can head home and end the night at any time by pressing Enter twice within 4 seconds. Everything you traced or discovered is already saved in your journal. The game sums up what the night brought, and the results menu offers Play again, Hear the results again, and Back to the title. Escape also returns to the title.

## The star journal

Star journal on the title menu reads every constellation you have traced, complete with the date and its lore, followed by your wonders, full moons, and wishes. Records gives your tallies, your nights on the hill, your best meteor watches, and your challenge results.

## Challenges

The Challenges menu offers 3 challenges that give every player the exact same sky, with this season's entire sky open. Each briefing lays out the goal and when the next one arrives, and Escape backs out without starting.

- Daily, Tonight's star hunt: track down 5 named stars one at a time. Each star is worth 100 points, minus 25 for each hint, and minus 50 if the guide tone led you there.
- Weekly, The week's patterns: trace 3 constellations. You earn 10 points per star and 100 per constellation, minus 10 per hint.
- Monthly, The grand tour: trace every constellation in the season's sky and find every wonder. Wonders are worth 150 points each.

Your result counts once you finish the goal or head home with Enter twice. Picking Leave the challenge from the pause menu does not count.

## Sound check and tutorial

Sound check on the title menu plays each sound you need under the sky, and Tutorial walks you through the basics hands-on.

## Controls

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's instructions follow your bindings.

- Left and Right arrows: sweep the listening point around the sky.
- Up and Down arrows: raise and lower the point.
- Space: trace the star under the point, log a wonder, or greet the moon. During a meteor watch, make a wish.
- Tab: toggle the guide tone on or off. During a meteor watch, read the shower's story.
- S: report where the point is, what it rests on, and any constellation you are partway through. Press it again straight away for tonight's progress, the guide tone, and the moon.
- R: repeat the last thing spoken.
- H: get a hint naming the next star to find and how many steps away it is.
- Enter twice: head home and end the night, with the second press within 4 seconds.
- Escape: pause. From the pause menu you can resume, open Settings or this help, go back to the title, or return to the lobby. During a challenge, the leave row is Leave the challenge.
- F1: read what the keys do on the current screen.
