# Stargazer

## About the game

Find stars in the night sky and trace each constellation in order to add it to your journal. Move a listening point across the sky to search. There is no time limit or way to lose.

## The listening point

Left and Right sweep the listening point round the sky, one step of 5 degrees at a time. Up and Down raise and lower it, from the horizon to straight overhead.

You start facing south, 40 degrees up. When you pass due north, east, south or west, the direction is spoken. S says where the point is, such as "Southwest, 40 degrees up".

## Stars near the point

You can hear stars within about 30 degrees of the listening point. Their sounds indicate direction, height and brightness. Sound check, on the title, lets you learn these cues.

When the point rests on a star, its name is spoken, such as "Betelgeuse, in Orion". A star that belongs to no pattern tonight is "a faint star". Faint stars are scattered differently each night.

## Tracing a constellation

Each constellation has its stars in an order, such as Orion: Betelgeuse, Bellatrix, Mintaka, Alnilam, Alnitak, Saiph, then Rigel. When the point rests on the star that is next to trace, you hear "next", such as "Bellatrix, next in Orion". Space traces it.

Space on a star of the right constellation but out of order names the star that comes next. When the last star is traced, the constellation plays its own tune, and the first time, it goes into your star journal with its lore.

## Hints and the guide tone

H names the next star to find and the way there in steps, such as "Bellatrix, next in Orion: 3 steps right, 1 step up". Hints finish a constellation you have begun, then point to ones not yet in your journal, then to the nearest.

Tab turns the guide tone on or off. It guides you toward the next star, indicating its direction and how close you are.

## The sky and the calendar

The sky follows the real calendar of the northern hemisphere's evening sky. Spring, from March to May, brings Leo, Bootes, Virgo and Cancer. Summer, from June to August, brings Lyra, Cygnus, Aquila, Scorpius and Sagittarius. Lyra's Vega, Cygnus's Deneb and Aquila's Altair make the Summer Triangle. Autumn, from September to November, brings Pegasus, Andromeda, Perseus and Aries.

Ursa Major, Ursa Minor, Cassiopeia, Cepheus and Draco circle the pole star and are up all year, in a different place each season.

## New constellations

You start with the brightest few: the Big Dipper's Ursa Major, Cassiopeia, and one or two of the season's own. When your journal holds 2 constellations, more rise, and more again at 5. They can rise in the middle of a night. Their names are spoken when they rise. To fill the whole journal, come back in every season.

## Wonders

Each season hides a few wonders: the Orion Nebula and the Pleiades in winter, the Beehive cluster in spring, the Ring Nebula and the heart of the Milky Way in summer, and the Andromeda Galaxy and the Double Cluster in autumn. Mizar and Alcor, in the Big Dipper's handle, are up all year.

A wonder can be detected when the listening point comes within about 2 steps. Rest the point on it and press Space to find it.

## The full moon

On a full moon night, the moon rises low in the east. Rest on it and press Space to greet it, and it goes into your journal by its old name, such as the Hunter's Moon in October. A second full moon in one month is a blue moon. Under a full moon the faint stars are fewer and quieter.

## Meteor showers

The year's great meteor showers fall on their real peak nights: the Quadrantids on 3 January, the Lyrids on 22 April, the Eta Aquariids on 6 May, the Perseids on 12 August, the Orionids on 21 October and the Leonids on 17 November. The peak counts from the day before to the day after, and for 3 days before that, a few early shooting stars fall.

On those nights the Meteor shower row on the title opens. Other nights it says locked, and its help names the next shower and how many days away it is.

## Making wishes

During a meteor watch you lie still. Press Space while a shooting star passes, or just after it fades, to make a wish. Fireballs pass more slowly. Tab tells the shower's story. Enter twice heads home, and your wishes are counted for that shower and year.

## Heading home

Enter twice heads home and ends the night at any time: the first press asks, the second within 4 seconds goes. Everything you traced or found is already in your journal. The game reads what the night brought, then the results menu offers Play again, Hear the results again, and Back to the title. Escape does the same as the last row.

## The star journal

Star journal, on the title, reads every constellation you have traced, with the date and its lore, then your wonders, full moons and wishes. Records reads the tallies, your nights on the hill and your best meteor watches, with your challenge results.

## Challenges

The Challenges menu holds 3 challenges that are the same for every player, under this season's whole sky. Each briefing gives the goal and when the next one comes. Escape on a briefing goes back without starting.

- Daily, Tonight's star hunt: find 5 named stars, one at a time. Each is worth 100 points, less 25 for each hint, and 50 if the guide tone led you there.
- Weekly, The week's patterns: trace 3 constellations. Each star is worth 10 points, each constellation 100, and each hint costs 10.
- Monthly, The grand tour: trace every constellation of the season and find each wonder. Wonders are worth 150.

The result counts when the goals are met, or when you head home with Enter twice. Leave the challenge, on the pause menu, does not count.

## Sound check and tutorial

Sound check, on the title, plays each sound you need under the sky. Tutorial explains the game a page at a time.

## Controls

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's spoken instructions follow your bindings.

- Left and Right arrows: sweep the listening point round the sky.
- Up and Down arrows: raise and lower the point.
- Space: trace the star the point rests on, find a wonder, or greet the moon. In a meteor watch, make a wish.
- Tab: the guide tone on or off. In a meteor watch, the shower's story.
- S: status. Where the point is, what it rests on, and any constellation you are part way through. Press it again straight away for tonight's progress, the guide tone and the moon.
- R: repeat the last thing said.
- H: hint. The next star to find, and the way there in steps.
- Enter twice: head home and end the night. The first press asks, the second within 4 seconds goes.
- Escape: pause. The pause menu can resume, open settings or this help, go back to the title, or go back to the lobby. During a challenge, its leave row is Leave the challenge.
- F1: what the keys do on the current screen.
