# Cupid's Quiver

## About the game

Shoot the paper hearts drifting above the rooftops. Aim, draw the bow and release your arrow. A round ends when the quiver is empty. On the rooftops you need enough hearts to advance; fall short and the evening ends.

## Aiming

Left and Right turn the bow 5 degrees a press, up to 70 degrees either way. The sky turns with you, so a heart straight in front of the bow sounds straight ahead, in the middle, and a heart off to one side sounds from that side. Quieter hearts are farther off. Up faces straight ahead again. Each step of the bow says your aim, such as "15 degrees right", unless aim call-outs are off.

## Drawing and shooting

Hold Space to draw the bow. It reaches full draw after nearly a second. Release after the full-draw cue to shoot. Release too soon and you keep the arrow. Down eases the string without shooting.

## Leading a heart

Hearts keep drifting while the arrow flies. A near heart is reached in a quarter of a second, a far one in nearly three quarters, so far hearts need the most lead. Aim a little ahead of a heart, in the way it is drifting. When a shot let go now would meet a target, the sights cue plays from it. H names the best target and the angle to meet it at.

## Sweetheart pairs

Hearts come in pairs: two hearts of one colour, red, pink, peach, violet or white, that hum the same note. The second heart of a pair drifts in a moment after the first, from either side. Hit both, one after the other, for a sweetheart pair: 50 bonus points. A miss, or another target hit in between, breaks the pair. S, Tab and H always name the colours, so you never need to tell the notes apart.

## Scoring

A near heart scores 10, a middle one 20 and a far one 30. Each hit in a row adds 5 more, up to 25 more. A sweetheart pair adds 50. A rose adds 25 to its distance points. A golden heart scores 100, and a love note 40.

## The rooftops and moonlight

The main game is six rooftops, from the bakery to the cathedral. Each round has a quiver of arrows and a number of hearts to hit to go on. Later roofs have more arrows, more hearts at once, faster drifting, more far hearts and a narrower aim. Clear all six in one evening to open moonlight: endless rounds under the moon, faster with each one, until a round falls short.

## Love notes

Now and then a folded love note drifts slowly over the rooftops. Hit it and the note is read out and kept, even if you leave the evening early. There are 24 to find. Love notes on the title reads every note you have kept, a page at a time.

## Valentine's Day

The game follows the real calendar. In the 7 days before the 14th of February, roses drift among the hearts, each worth 25 more. On Valentine's Day itself, from the day before to the day after so every time zone is covered, two more modes open. Valentine's night is four rooftops under a full moon, with golden hearts that drift fast and score 100. For two is the same night for two players, who take turns with one bow, one arrow each, and share the score. Either one leaves a keepsake for that year in the keepsake box on the title. The rest of the year these rows are locked, and their help says how many days are left.

## The practice field

The practice field has one slow heart at a time, never far, and endless arrows. Nothing is saved. Practise turning, drawing and leading a heart. Escape pauses.

## Challenges

Every player gets the same challenges. The daily is today's sky: one long round of 15 arrows with a twist, such as a breeze that speeds the hearts, a far sky, a crowded sky, or sweetheart pairs worth double. The weekly is three rooftops, each busier than the last, new each Monday. The monthly is five rounds under the full moon, with roses. Every round is played and the points are added. Your result counts when the last round ends. Leave the challenge, on the pause menu, does not count, so you can try again later.

## Spoken call-outs

Two rows in Settings turn spoken call-outs on and off, from the title or from the pause menu during a run. The Spoken aim setting says your aim each time the bow turns, such as "15 degrees right". With Spoken aim off, each step only ticks, and S still gives your aim. The Spoken targets setting names each target as it drifts in, such as "Pink heart, from the left." With Spoken targets off, you hear only the hums, and Tab still lists the sky. The first time you meet each kind of target or cue, it is explained in words, once. The tutorial explains the bow and the sights cue, so after it play does not explain them again.

## Sound check

Sound check on the title plays the cues you need during play.

## Controls

Left and Right: turn the bow 5 degrees.

Up: face straight ahead.

Down: ease the string without shooting, or count your arrows.

Space: hold to draw, and let go at full draw to shoot. Between rooftops, climb to the next one.

Enter: between rooftops, climb to the next one.

Tab: list everything in the sky, left to right.

S: status. Whose turn in the game for two, your aim, how far the bow is drawn, arrows left, and hearts hit and needed. Press S again straight away for the round, points and how many targets are in the sky.

R: repeat the last announcement.

H: a hint, naming the best target and how far to turn for it.

F1: help for what you are doing right now. F2 stops speech.

Escape: pause, with Resume, Settings, Help, Back to the title, and Back to the lobby. In a challenge, Back to the title is Leave the challenge.

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's spoken instructions follow your bindings.
