# Fireside Patience

## About the game

Cabin patience: move all 52 cards onto four suit foundations, ace to king. Every relaxed deal is winnable; undo unlimited; no clock, no move limit, no way to lose.

## The row of places

One row of 12: piles 1–7, then stock, then foundations (hearts, diamonds, clubs, spades). Left/Right move; each place speaks its top card. A soft chime marks the step from the piles to the stock, and from the stock to the foundations. At either end of the row, a dull tap tells you there is no further to go.

## Piles

Each pile starts with face-down cards and one face-up card on top. A pile builds down in alternating colours: a black 7 goes on a red 8, and a red queen on a black king. Only a king goes on an empty pile. When a pile's last face-up card leaves, the card under it turns face up with a flip, and you hear its name. Up goes one card deeper into the face-up run, and Down comes back toward the top card.

## Moving cards

Space picks up the card under you, with every card on top of it. Move to where the cards go, and Space again places them. A soft tap means they went. A muffled thud means they cannot go there, and you hear the rule, such as "The 9 of spades cannot go on the 9 of hearts. It needs a red 10." You keep holding the cards, so you can try another place. While you hold cards, a place they fit says "Fits here." Holding one card on the wrong foundation, you hear where it goes, such as "Goes to spades." Space on the place they came from puts them back.

## The stock

Tab turns one card up from the stock. At the stock's place, Space picks up the turned-up card. When the stock runs out, Tab turns the turned-up cards back over to use again, as often as you like. Tab while you hold cards puts them back first. In draw three, Tab turns 3 cards at a time, and only the top one can be played.

## Foundations

Each suit has its own foundation, built up one card at a time from the ace to the king. Space on any foundation sends a single card to its own suit. Once every card is face up and the stock is used, the rest go up on their own. A bright chime marks a complete suit, and a warm flourish the win.

## Undo and getting stuck

Backspace takes back your last move, as many times as you like, face-down cards and all. While you hold cards, Backspace puts them back. If no move is left that helps, the game says so. Every relaxed deal can be won from the start, so undo far enough and a way through is always there. For a fresh deal, press Escape, then Back to the title, then Play.

## Hints

H names the next move, such as "The 4 of clubs in pile 2 can go on the 5 of hearts in pile 6." It follows a line that wins the deal from where you are, when it can find one, so taking every hint never leads into a dead end. When the card it wants is deeper in the stock, the hint says to turn it. Hints cost nothing.

## Status and the table

S gives the cards you hold and where you are. Press S again straight away for the cards on the foundations, the cards left to turn in the stock, the face-down cards and your moves, always in that order. R reads the whole table: the top card of each pile, the stock and the four foundations.

## Scoring and records

A move is any card move or any turn of the stock. Undo takes a move back, so it does not count. A won deal scores 1000 points, less 1 for every move. Records keep the relaxed deals you have won, your fewest moves and your best score.

## Challenges

Every player gets the same cards, and every challenge deal can be won. The daily, Tonight's deal, is one draw one deal, new each day. The weekly, Draw three week, is one draw three deal, new each Monday. The monthly, Five by the fire, is five draw one deals in a row, with the scores added. Your result counts when the last card goes up. Leave the challenge, on the pause menu, does not count, so you can try again later.

## Calendar events

Rabbit rabbit deal: on the 1st of every month, when an old charm says "rabbit rabbit" for a lucky month, the Rabbit rabbit deal row on the title opens. On other days it stays in its place, locked, and says when it opens. It is one deal for every player that day, and it can always be won. Turning the stock costs no moves, so the score counts only the cards you move, and S says "turns are free". The fire burns low, and an owl calls outside in place of the usual fireside. It is scored on a board of its own. Once the day is over, Play again on the results says the Rabbit rabbit deal has ended and takes you back to the title.

## Sounds

Each suit has its own soft card sound, a paper slide with a short tone: a warm low bell for hearts, a bright high bell for diamonds, a soft wooden knock for clubs and a deep wooden knock for spades. You hear it with the card's name when you reach or pick up a card. A flip is a card turning face up. A soft tap places cards, and a muffled thud refuses them. A short shuffle turns the stock. A swish takes back a move. Under it all, the fire crackles and a clock ticks, kept quiet under speech. The sound check on the title plays each one with its name.

## Controls

Left and Right: move along the piles, the stock and the foundations.

Up and Down: move deeper into a pile's face-up cards, and back toward the top card.

Space: pick up the cards under you, or place the cards you hold.

Tab: turn the stock.

Backspace: take back the last move, or put back the cards you hold. This key belongs to Fireside Patience and cannot be changed in Settings.

Home and End: jump to the first pile, or to the last foundation. These keys belong to Fireside Patience too.

S: what you hold and where you are. Press it twice for the rest of the table's count.

R: read the whole table.

H: a useful move.

F1: help for what you are doing right now. F2 stops speech.

Escape: pause, with Resume, Settings, Help, Back to the title, and Back to the lobby. In a challenge, Back to the title is Leave the challenge.

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's spoken instructions follow your bindings.
