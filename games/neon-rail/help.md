# Neon Rail: Black Sky Run

## About the game

You are a rogue courier skimming the dead transit rails of Black Sky City in a stolen combat skimmer. Your craft rockets forward across three lanes while you weave into the path of flow traffic, dodge hazards, blast drones with your rail gun, and boost straight through full blockades.

Make it to extraction to finish the run. If your hull drops to 0 or network stress climbs to 100, the run ends.

## Arrivals and call-outs

Every approaching arrival is placed relative to your current lane. Straight ahead means your lane, while left or right means one or two lanes over. As you shift lanes, those positions shift around you.

Spoken call-outs are on by default, so each arrival is named as it appears, like "Debris left." You can turn Spoken call-outs off in Settings from the title or the pause menu if you want to fly without them. The hint key always tells you what is coming next.

Use Sound check from the title to practise recognising each lane position and gameplay cue.

## Traffic you join

Move into the lane of friendly traffic or pickups before they reach you.

- Flow vehicle: meeting it scores points and eases stress. Missing it raises stress.
- Fast passer: you get a short warning before it tears past. Meet it in its lane or have a slowdown pulse active, or it clips the corridor for hull damage and stress.
- Priority vehicle: meet it in its lane, or cover it with priority override or a slowdown pulse. Delaying priority traffic raises stress sharply.
- Repair pickup: restores part of your hull.
- Salvage: adds to your score, which becomes credits at the debrief.

If you miss a pickup, it is simply gone. The game announces "Repair pickup missed" or "Salvage missed", with no penalty.

## Hazards you avoid

Shift out of a hazard's lane before it arrives, or punch through it with boost.

- Debris: dodge around it, or boost straight through for a boost smash bonus.
- Drone: shoot it down with the rail gun, dodge it, or ram it with boost.
- Heavy blocker: dodge past it, or boost into it to force it open for big points.
- Unstable vehicle: shoot it, dodge it, or boost through. Even when you dodge clear, its turbulence adds a little stress.
- Full blockade: spans all three lanes, so there is no way around it. Hit boost on the blockade cue, just under a second before impact.

## Stress and pressure

Network stress runs from 0 to 100. Mistakes push it up, and mistakes in a row push it up faster. Clean passes, pickups, emergency tools, and sector clears all bring stress back down, and calm flow eases it slowly over time.

- The rising-pressure warning begins at stress 35.
- At stress 75 or higher, the high-stress warning repeats every five seconds.
- A recovery cue marks when stress drops back out of danger.

## Emergency tools

- Boost: gives you just under a second of breach power. It smashes debris, rams drones, forces heavy blockers open, punches through unstable vehicles, and breaches full blockades. It takes five seconds to recharge, and a ready cue marks when it is back.
- Rail gun: locks onto the nearest drone or unstable vehicle in any lane and destroys it for 80 points. It only locks during the final second of an approach, slightly less when the target is one or two lanes over. Fire too early or at an empty rail and you get "No lock", with a short cooldown under a second.
- Slowdown pulse: gives you two and a half seconds where fast passers, priority traffic, and heavy blockers are eased past, and drops stress right away. Charges are limited, with a fourteen-second recharge.
- Priority override: holds priority traffic for you for three and a half seconds no matter which lane you are in, and drops stress right away. Charges are limited.

## Scoring

You earn points every second for distance, plus points for every clean pass, dodge, smash, pickup, and kill, along with a bonus at the end of each sector. Four clean plays in a row raise your multiplier, up to five times, and each sector clear bumps it up too. Any hit resets the multiplier to one. Reaching extraction pays 500 times your multiplier.

## Pace

The rail pauses briefly while a call-out or message is being spoken, holding arrivals, tool timers, and the sector clock in place. Asking for the same line over and over stops holding the rail after a while. This works the same way at every pace and in every mode.

Pace in Settings lets you choose how fast the rail runs: relaxed, normal, or quick. Normal is the default and sits just below quick. Quick is the fastest, while relaxed runs at half speed.

Pace changes take effect on your next run, and a run keeps its starting pace through every sector.

Rank, credits, parts, intel, unlocks, and story progress all count at every pace. Relaxed scores are tracked on their own boards, including challenges and special nights, while normal and quick share their scores.

## Modes

- Play: your very first run is the guided training route. After that, Play launches a Black Sky Run.
- Black Sky Run: five sectors from departure to extraction. After each sector you pick one upgrade from three, then choose your next route from up to three options. The fifth sector is the extraction sprint.
- Challenge corridor: the original authored two-minute run across six fixed sectors, identical every time. Learn the pattern and chase a high score.
- Endless rail: unlocks after your first Black Sky extraction. Fly sector after sector, each tougher than the last. Between sectors, choosing End run here at the bottom of the upgrade menu banks it as a finished run with full pay and records your sector reached. Quitting from the pause menu counts nothing.
- Cold start: unlocks alongside the endless rail. You fly a bare skimmer with no rank bonuses or workshop parts across nine sectors, choosing upgrades and routes between them, with one life. Every route is open. It keeps its own score board and pays no credits, parts, or intel, and your rank stays put, though routes cleared, runs lost, and kills still count toward the story archive. Records tracks cold starts finished and your furthest sector.
- Practice run: flies the guided one-minute route again, pausing with a tutorial page as each new kind of traffic appears. It pays no rewards after the first clear.
- Challenges: daily, weekly, and monthly runs on the same rail for every pilot.

Each mode tracks its own best score, shown on its title row and in Records.

## Challenges

Open Challenges from the title menu. Each row gives the period, a summary of the run, and your current record, either not played yet or your best score and streak. Press F1 on any row for the full rule and reset timer. Enter opens the briefing with the rule, your record, the reset time, and the controls, then starts the run. Escape on the briefing backs out without starting.

Every pilot flies the exact same rail in each period on a standard skimmer: 100 hull, two slowdown pulses, and two priority overrides. Workshop parts and rank bonuses stay in the hangar, though you still earn credits and parts at the debrief and cleared routes count toward the archive.

- Daily, the sector sprint: one 60-second sector at fourth-sector difficulty along a daily route, either a standard rush, an ambush corridor, or the storm rail. Clear the sector to win. Each day adds one twist, announced in the briefing and at launch:
  - Dense traffic: arrivals come more than a quarter closer together.
  - Boost offline: the boost key does nothing. Heavy blockers replace full blockades, so dodge or shoot everything.
  - Fog: every warning is a quarter shorter, so shift as soon as an arrival appears.
- Weekly, the long haul: three sectors on a fixed route ending in the extraction sprint, with its own weekly twist. You fit an upgrade after the first two sectors, while the route ahead stays locked to one choice.
- Monthly, the Black Sky event, Blackout Convoy: three evacuation convoy sectors followed by the extraction sprint. Priority vehicles become evacuation trains, called out as "Evacuation train". Each train you clear pays triple, while each delay costs double stress. Stress starts at 30, and you carry three priority overrides. The briefing reads the event's story log, and finishing the event adds that log to your story archive.

During a challenge, S includes the active twist or rule, and F1 adds the challenge rule as its final page.

A challenge counts as soon as the run ends, win or lose. You get your result, whether you beat your best, your streak, and when the next challenge arrives. Leaving from the pause menu does not count. Only a higher score replaces your best, while any finished run keeps your streak alive. Daily challenges reset at midnight UTC, weekly ones on Monday, and monthly ones on the first of the month. Afterward, the results menu lets you fly the same rail again or head back to the challenges list.

## Special nights

Ghost cars run from 17 October through Halloween and the day after. In the guided run, Black Sky Runs, the endless rail, the challenge corridor, and cold starts, flow vehicles turn into ghost cars. Meet them just like normal flow traffic for the same points. The briefing announces them, S names the night, and the hint key calls them ghost cars. They do not appear in daily, weekly, or monthly challenges.

Friday night run opens on the last Friday of every month as its own row on the title menu. On other days the row stays locked and tells you when it returns. You fly three sectors built from that Friday's date on a standard skimmer, the same rail for every pilot: a salvage stretch, one more route, then extraction. You fit an upgrade between sectors, though the route is fixed. Salvage appears as cargo crates worth double. It keeps its own board, pays no credits, parts, or intel, and leaves your rank and story untouched. Once Friday ends, Play again on the results screen lets you know and will not start another run.

## Routes and upgrades

Between sectors you pick your next route. The three options always include whichever open route you have cleared the fewest times:

- Standard rush: mixed traffic with normal pay.
- Salvage stretch: extra pickups and fewer threats, for a smaller sector bonus.
- Ambush corridor: heavy waves of drones and unstable vehicles, paying one and a half times the bonus.
- Data relay: lighter traffic, plus it recovers an intel fragment.
- Storm rail: fast traffic with short warnings, paying nearly double the bonus. Unlock it in the workshop from rank 3.

Upgrades stay fitted for the rest of the run: shield plating, boost coils, heavy rounds for the rail gun, a hull patch, extra pulse or override cells, a near-miss amplifier, a stress damper, and a pickup magnet. Cold starts unlock four extra upgrades for every run's pool. Reach sector 4 for Hard lock, which extends your rail gun lock window. Reach sector 7 for Scrap mesh, so repairs ease more stress and salvage mends 2 hull. Finish a cold start for the Dead man's switch, which saves you from one hull breach per run at 20 hull. Finish three for Hot chain, raising your multiplier every 3 clean plays instead of 4. Pilot progress lists them all. Press S during an intermission to check your skimmer, or H for advice.

## Progress, the workshop and the archive

Every debrief pays out, even after a failed run. You earn credits from your score and finished runs, parts from cleared sectors and finished runs, and intel fragments from Black Sky extractions and data relays. Salvage pays through your score. Endless runs pay credits on their first 40,000 points. Your pilot rank climbs at 1, 4, 10, and 20 Black Sky extractions, each adding a permanent bonus: extra hull, an extra override, an extra slowdown pulse, and then a doubled starting multiplier.

The workshop trades credits and parts for permanent upgrades: a reinforced frame, spare charges, boost tuning, storm charts, a pickup magnet, and stress dampers. Higher ranks unlock more gear. Rank 2 opens an armoured nose to halve blockade damage and long-range sensors to announce repairs and salvage half a second sooner. Rank 3 opens a capacitor bank for longer boost and a heat sink for a faster rail gun. Rank 4 opens a shield emitter for one shield charge per run. Rank 5 opens a chain governor so hits halve your multiplier instead of resetting it. Two more parts unlock through the story: the Relay tuner, letting data relays recover 2 intel fragments, opens with the signal's first log at rank 4, and the Hunter jammer, announcing drones and unstable vehicles half a second sooner, opens with Security's last log. Clear the storm rail in a cold start to get Storm charts fitted free. Fifty rail gun kills earn the Ace gunner licence.

The story archive is grouped into strands. The data core's twelve logs form the main thread: early logs unlock as you finish runs, last longer, and reach extraction, while later ones open one per run as you gather intel fragments. Each side strand unlocks one log per run from a specific style of play: the unions from salvage stretches, the hunters from standard rushes, security from ambush corridors, the weather service from the storm rail, the signal from data relays, the Black box from lost runs, the deep rail from endless rail depth, the hangar from your own record, and Origin from extractions after finishing the data core. The Blackout Convoy event log opens once you complete a monthly Black Sky event. Logs found mid-run pause the rail while they play; the rest appear at the debrief and in the archive on the title menu, where every strand explains how it unlocks.

## The title and results

From the title menu you can choose Play, Challenge corridor, Endless rail, and Cold start, with locked modes marked until you open them. Below those are Challenges, Practice run, Tutorial, Help, and Sound check. You also have Records for best scores, pilot rank, credits, bonuses, and challenge results, plus Workshop, Story archive, Settings for Spoken call-outs and Pace, and Back to the parlor. On your first visit, the title starts on Tutorial.

After a debrief, the results menu offers Play again or Try again, Hear the results again, Pilot progress, Archive for your story logs, and Back to the title. After the guided run, Next launches straight into a Black Sky Run. After a challenge, the top row flies the same challenge again and the bottom row returns to the challenges menu. Escape acts like the last row.

## Pages

Story, tutorial, and debrief text is read one page at a time. Enter moves to the next unread page, Up and Down step back and forth through pages, R repeats the current page, and Escape skips to the end. On pre-run briefings, Escape backs out without starting the run.

## Controls

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's instructions follow your bindings.

- Left and Right arrows: shift one lane. A and D also work.
- Space: boost for just under a second. Recharges in five seconds.
- Tab: fire the rail gun at the nearest drone or unstable vehicle as it closes in. F also works.
- Down arrow: trigger a slowdown pulse. X also works.
- Up arrow: activate priority override. P also works.
- S: check your lane, hull, and stress. Press it again right away for tool states, and a third time for the sector, time left, score, and multiplier.
- R: repeat the last call-out or message.
- H: check what arrives next, where it is, how soon it hits, and what to do.
- Escape: pause, with Resume, Settings, Help, Back to the title, and Back to the lobby. In a challenge, Back to the title becomes Leave the challenge.
- F1: help for the current screen. During a run it pauses the rail and reads the controls a page at a time. F2 stops speech.
