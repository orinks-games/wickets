# Liberty Lights

## About the game

You are the fireworks director for a Fourth of July celebration. Each level plays you a show of fireworks, and you launch it back: the right firework, in the right place, at the right moment. Twenty levels, a freestyle mode where you compose your own show, and a grand finale for those who clear them all. A level clears at 60 points or more out of 100.

The title: Play begins the next level you have not cleared. Grand finale opens once level 20 is cleared. Freestyle show composes, saves and replays your own shows. Level select plays any of the twenty levels. Independence night opens on the Fourth of July. Challenges holds the daily, weekly and monthly challenges. Practice room is a launch range with nothing to score. Tutorial is a short spoken walkthrough, Help opens this guide, and Sound check plays each sound you need in a show, by name. Records reads back your best scores. Settings changes speech, volume and controls.

## A round

1. Press Space, the main action, to hear the show. It starts with a count-in: three wooden ticks half a second apart, then a brighter tick called the go. The go is time zero.
2. The fireworks go up. Two seconds after the last one, a fuse fizzes and you hear "Your turn".
3. Press Space again for your own count-in: the same three ticks and the go. Launch each firework at the moment it went up in the show, measured from the go.
4. Press Enter when you are finished. The crowd reacts and you hear your score.

Once the show has played, Tab, the second action, plays it again. That clears anything you have launched so far.

## Choosing with Up and Down

While you launch, Space, the main action, does whatever you have chosen, and Up and Down step through the choices, each spoken as you reach it: boom, crackle, whistle, sparkle, take back the last firework, and finish the show. It starts on boom, and a number key chooses its firework as it launches it, so Space then launches that kind again. This is how you play on a controller: A launches, Up and Down on the d-pad choose, and holding Left or Right on the d-pad aims. Enter on the keyboard still finishes, and Backspace still takes back.

## Fireworks

- 1, boom: a deep bass thump that rolls away.
- 2, crackle: a high scatter of tiny pops.
- 3, whistle: a rising whistle that ends in a small pop.
- 4, sparkle: a soft glassy shimmer.

The number keys on the keypad work too.

## Left, center and right

From level 11, fireworks also go up on the left or the right. Hold the Left arrow or A while you press a number to launch on the left; hold the Right arrow or D for the right. With neither held, the firework goes up in the center. Pressing Left or Right on its own plays a small tick on that side, so you can check where you are aiming.

## Scoring

Each firework in the show is matched with whichever of your launches fits it best:

- 50 points for the right kind of firework.
- 25 points for the right place.
- Up to 25 points for timing: 25 within a tenth of a second, 20 within 0.3 seconds, 15 within half a second, 10 within a second, 5 within two seconds.

Every firework too many or too few takes 20 points off. The total is your score out of 100. 60 or more clears the level.

The crowd tells you how it went: a cheer for 80 or more, an "ooh" for 60 or more, an "aah" for 40 or more, and a confused murmur below that. The better the show, the louder they are.

## Combos and show points

A firework matched almost perfectly, with the right kind and place and within 0.3 seconds, starts a combo, and each next firework in the show matched as well adds to it. Each step of a combo is worth bonus points: 50 for the second in a row, 100 for the third, and so on. Combo bonuses are paid only when the show clears, at 60 or more. You hear a bright chime with your results when a combo paid.

Show points are your score times ten, plus combo bonuses. Every level keeps its best show points. Your run total is the points of every level cleared in a row from where you started, and it keeps a best too. Replaying a level starts it again from the run total you had before it, so a level counts once. Saving a new freestyle show clears the best score of the one it replaces.

A hint takes 15 points off your score.

## Levels

- Levels 1 to 5: one firework. Match its kind and its moment.
- Levels 6 to 10: two fireworks.
- Levels 11 to 15: three fireworks, on the left, center or right.
- Levels 16 to 20: four to eight fireworks, and some go up early, overlapping the one before.

After level 1, a show may start up to two seconds after the go, so listen for when the first firework goes up, not just what it is.

## Challenges

Challenges on the title, or Challenges in the lobby, holds three challenges that are the same for every player and change on their own: the daily each day, the weekly each Monday, the monthly on the first of the month. Each row names the challenge and what it is, then your result so far and your streak. Choosing one reads a short briefing, with the twist and how long until the next one. Enter on its last page starts the first show, and Escape goes back without starting. A challenge counts when its last show is over. Leaving by Leave the challenge on the pause menu, or by Back to the challenges between shows, does not count it. Your score is the show points of every show added up, and the results say your result, your best and your streak.

- Daily, tonight's show: one show from the harder half of the campaign, levels 11 to 20, under a twist.
- Weekly, the five-show gauntlet: five shows in a row, each bigger than the last, under the week's twist. A show under 60 percent ends the gauntlet; its points still count.
- Monthly, the festival: a themed festival, such as Independence Day in July, with two acts and a 14-firework finale. Every act is played, whatever it scores. The festival celebrates one firework, its signature, and its shows are full of them: on a show that clears, each signature firework matched near-perfectly, with the right kind and place within 0.3 seconds, adds 100 bonus points.

The twists:

- Quickstep: the shows play at double speed, fireworks a second apart instead of two.
- Tough crowd: timing points only within 0.3 seconds; any firework further off earns none.
- One shot: each show plays only once, with no hearing it again and no hints.
- Night fog: the show's fireworks sound far off and quiet, though still on their side; your own launches sound as usual. The sound check has a fog boom to hear first.

A chime opens each challenge show, and a fanfare plays when a challenge ends with your best result on it.

## Results

After a level that clears: Next level, Play again, Hear the results again, Compare, and Back to the title. Play again plays the level with a new show. Hear the results again reads the result, then how well each firework matched. Compare plays the show, then your own launches, each after a count-in. After a level that does not clear, Try again plays a new show and Practice this level plays the very same one again. Escape does what Back to the title does.

After a challenge show, the results end with Back to the challenges. Between the shows of a weekly or monthly challenge, the first row goes on to the next show.

## Freestyle

Compose a show of your own: launch with 1 to 4, aiming with Left and Right, with no score and no clock to beat. Your show's time starts at your first launch. Backspace takes back the last firework; Enter finishes. Afterwards you can hear your show with the crowd watching, save it, or choose Play again to start another. A saved show can be heard again from the freestyle menu, or played with Match your saved show, scored like a level.

## Grand finale

Clear level 20 to open the grand finale: an endless run of shows, each longer and faster than the last, with fireworks all over the sky. It goes on until a round scores under 60. Your finale total is kept as a best score.

## Independence night

On the Fourth of July, the Independence night row on the title opens a show built for the day. It counts as the day from the 3rd to the 5th of July, so it is open on the Fourth wherever you are. The rest of the year the row reads Independence night, locked, and F1 on it says how many days are left.

The night has four acts, each played whatever it scores:

1. The opening salute: six booms, left and right, then two in the center. It is the same every year.
2. Stars and stripes: sparkles and crackles in turn, all over the sky.
3. Whistles over the river: ten fireworks, most of them whistles, some going up early.
4. The grand finale: sixteen quick fireworks of every kind, then a closing salute of four booms, left, center, right and center.

The boom is the night's signature firework. On an act that clears, every boom matched near-perfectly, with the right place and within 0.3 seconds, adds 100 bonus points. A salute of three drum booms and a trumpet call opens the night.

In the week before the Fourth, Enter on the row plays a rehearsal of the opening salute, so you can learn it early.

When the last act is over, the night is kept as that year's keepsake: your best show points and the most acts you cleared. Records reads every year's keepsake.

## Practice room

The practice room is a launch range with nothing to score. Launch with 1 to 4 or Space, aiming with Left and Right, and each firework is named after it goes up. Escape pauses.

## Sound check

Each sound you need in a show, by name. On each firework row, Enter plays it in the center and Left and Right play it on that side. There are rows for the aim tick, the launch thump, the count-in tick, the go, the fuse that means your turn, the four crowd reactions, the combo, undo, the choice tick and a boom through the night fog. Two keys belong to this game alone and cannot be changed in Settings: Enter finishes your show, and Backspace takes back your last firework.

## Controller

On a controller, A is the main action: it hears the show, starts your count-in, and launches the firework you have chosen. Up and Down on the d-pad choose a firework, take back or finish; hold Left or Right on the d-pad to launch on that side. X hears the show again, Y gives the status, the left bumper gives a hint, a click of the right stick repeats the instructions, Back reads help and B or Start pauses.

## Controls

- Space: hear the show; start your count-in.
- 1 to 4: launch a boom, crackle, whistle or sparkle.
- Hold Left or A, Right or D: launch on that side.
- Enter: finish your show and score it.
- Backspace: take back your last firework.
- Tab: hear the show again.
- R: repeat the instructions for where you are.
- S: the level, how many fireworks, how many you have launched and the time since the go. Press it again straight away for the level's style, any twist, and your points so far.
- H: a hint, the show described in words, for 15 points.
- F1: help for the current screen.
- Up and Down: choose what Space does while you launch, a firework, take back, or finish.
- Escape: pause, with help and a way back to the title, or out of a challenge.

These are the default keys. If you change them in Settings, the game's spoken instructions follow your bindings.
